# CreateStoryline — Product & System Specification

**Status:** normative draft. This document is the build contract. Requirement ids (`P1`, `D-3`, `T-1`, `L-4`, …) are stable: assign work by id, cite ids in commit messages and tests.

**Audience:** a swarm of agents building the Server and the Dashboard in parallel. Each workstream (§12) is independently buildable against the interfaces in §5–§9. Where a section says MUST, a build that omits it is wrong; where it says SHOULD, it is expected but a reasoned deviation is acceptable if recorded in §14.

---

## 1. Product thesis

An **Author** sits at the top of a story stack and wants many stories moving at once. They write the thing only they can write — the **Seed**, the intention of the story, and later the **lock** on each phase — and then the system does the hundreds of small, tedious, consistency-preserving jobs that make a story hold together, forever, until the Author says stop.

The system is a **loop engine**. It constantly scans every Story for unfinished work, and for each piece of work it **opens a fresh thread** (one employee with fresh context), hands it the story id and one job, and tears it down when it reports. The Author's screen is a queue of decisions, not a chat box.

**Rapid** is the product goal. Rapid means:

- **R1** — the Author never faces a blank page. Employees draft; the Author signs.
- **R2** — a decision costs the Author one interaction (pick, approve, reject, one line of direction).
- **R3** — work happens in parallel across stories and across chapters.
- **R4** — nothing waits on the Author that does not need to.

**Authorship is preserved.** The Author is the only terminal authority: the loop ends when the Author stops it, a chapter is done when the Author says it is done, the Seed is untouchable by agents, and so is the Summary once the Author has locked it. Speed that costs the Author their book is not speed.

**The target is fidelity, not quality.** The objective is _a story this Author would have written by hand_ — not the best possible story, which is subjective and unachievable on purpose. Every template is a piece of the Author's own judgment, automated: what they like in prose, what they notice, what they would fix. A system that produces a technically better book that isn't theirs has failed. This is a requirement, not a philosophy (§5.3).

**The product is three phases, each ending in a lock.** Idea → **Summary** → **Plotlines** → **Chapters**. Each phase is a crew working until its Goal passes, then stopping for the Author's signature; the signature locks the artifact, and the next phase builds only on locked ground. The loop does not run endlessly by design: it runs _to_ a sign-off, not _until_ it gives up (§6.3a, §6.6).

**There is no deadline.** Runs may take days. Nothing in this product is gated on tokens or wall-clock (§11). The only failure the loop recognises is **going in circles**, and its response is to stop and tell the Author, not to give up.

## 2. Principles

These are load-bearing. Every later section derives from them.

**P1 — The Dashboard owns memory. Threads are stateless workers.**
Every Run opens a brand-new thread with no history. Therefore **the only memory in the system is the Dashboard's durable store.** An employee acting as an agent that cannot write a fact into the store has not produced it. A thread that dies mid-run loses nothing except its own uncommitted output.

**P2 — Fresh context every turn.**
A Run MUST never reuse a previous run's thread or transcript. This is the anti-context-rot mechanism: reviewers are independent because they have never seen the last review. It also means every turn pays a full re-read, which is why the **Digest** (§4.8) is mandatory rather than an optimisation.

**P3 — A story is its records.**
Nothing that is not in the store is part of the story: not a chat message, not an agent's reasoning, not a run's stdout. Prose lives in `chapters`. Structure lives in `plotlines`. Judgment lives in `verdicts` and `findings`. Direction lives in `tasks`.

**P4 — Every unit carries a contract, and the loop asks one question.**
A chapter carries a **Plotline**: what must happen in that chapter. The loop's question is never "is this good?" but **"does this artifact deliver its contract?"** — answerable by a fresh reviewer with a cited line of evidence.

**P5 — The idea is sacred; the Summary becomes sacred at the lock.**
The Author's **idea** is the seed and is read-only for every agent, forever. The **Summary** is Phase 1's _output_: five named groups — premise, promise, theme, tone, ending — each written and revised on its own line, composed into the Overview the loop measures everything against, and **locked** by the Author (§4.19, R1/R2). A Writer pass rewrites the groups a defect named, and what it accepted becomes the current text the moment it is accepted — the text it replaces goes into that group's history, where it stays (R2/R5). Before the lock the Summary is a draft like any other; after it, it is law, and the only way in is the Author unlocking, which backtracks (§6.3a). (A book whose `summary_mode` is `'sampling'` — every Story written before 2026-11-24 unless the Author imported it — still runs the older whole-Summary round: N competing samples, Readers screen them, the engine keeps the least-issue one and deletes the rest, §16.)

**P6 — Pass/fail criteria, not scores.**
A fresh context grading 1–10 is noise. A boolean with a citation is stable. The Author controls the goal by editing a list of criteria and a threshold, never by dragging a quality slider.

**P7 — Escalate, never silently halt.**
When a loop cannot proceed — no progress, a contradiction only a human can resolve — it parks a **question for the Author**. It does not stop and go quiet, and it does not spin.

**P8 — The Author can always pull the cord.**
A Stop on a Story or a global Stop halts dispatch immediately and kills in-flight runs. A stopped Story never auto-resumes.

**P9 — Agents propose against Author prose.**
An agent never silently overwrites text the Author wrote. It files a proposal. (See §7 permissions.) This is the one respect in which the Writer's monopoly (P10) is limited: the Author's own words outrank the Writer's.
**P9a — The Author writes prose too, in the UI, and only while the Story is paused.**
The chapter editor is not a read-only view: the Author edits the book with their own hands. To keep that from colliding with a crew mid-sentence, the edit requires the Story to be `stopped` (D-37). The pause is the lock; the resume is the hand-back.

**P10 — The Writer writes everything.**
Only the **Writer** employee kind writes story artifacts: the Summary, the plotlines, chapters, prose, cast, ledger. (Two named exceptions, and only two: the **Digest** is not part of the book but a derived index of it, and the engine builds it from commits — D-12a; and in phase ① the engine persists each validated structured answer of a tool-less run on the same engine-writes principle — a `GroupDraft` install across the groups of the Summary on a grouped book (§4.19, R5), a `SummarySample` `revisions` row on a legacy one (§16.5). A run authored the text; no tool wrote it.) Every other employee — **Editor**, **Reader**, **Checker**, or **Researcher** — reads, judges, files findings, opens tasks, proposes Ideas, writes Knowledge, and requests the Author; none of them writes a story artifact. A checker that thinks it can improve a sentence files the task and lets the Writer write it. This is what keeps a hundred independent fresh reviewers from producing a hundred different prose styles: one voice, many auditors.

**P11 — Every change is attributable.**
Every mutation records who made it and carries a one-line `why`. The Author must be able to see, for any paragraph, which run changed it and for what reason — and undo it.

**P12 — Templates are the Author, not best practice.**
A Writer, Editor, or Reader role in a template encodes _this Author's_ taste and _this Author's_ habits — the prose they like, the things they look out for, the calls they would make. A template is not a generic expert; it is the Author's own editorial judgment, externalised and automated. Built-in templates are starting points to be edited into the Author's likeness, never authorities to be deferred to.

**P13 — Revisions are ephemeral; one survives.**
Multiple attempts at the same chapter or plotline exist **at the same time**, side by side, and are compared by an Editor (on a legacy `'sampling'` Summary the samples are ranked by the engine, §16.6). When the Editor selects one — or merges several, or on a legacy book the engine picks — **every non-selected revision is deleted.** Losing the alternatives is deliberate: a pile of rejected drafts is exactly the context rot that P2 exists to prevent, and a fresh run that reads four versions of chapter 7 has already lost the thread. The chosen text is the only thing that persists; the reasoning for the choice persists as a record, not as the drafts. **The grouped Summary is the one place this principle does not delete anything** (R2, §4.19): a superseded group text is `archived` rather than destroyed, because a grouped install has no alternative on offer for anyone to be confused by — every reader, prompt and review reads the composed Overview, which is one text per group — and the history is where the Author's "what did it say before?" is answered without a journal dig. P2's rule is about what a fresh context READS, and a grouped reader still reads exactly one thing.

**P14 — A Story has a status, and the loop is allowed to say "I'm stuck."**
A Story is `ready` (employees may work on it), `pending` (the loop has stopped dispatching and the Author is the next mover), or `completed` (the Author has accepted the book as it stands, leftovers and all). `pending` has flavours and they are not the same news: **awaiting the Author** — a phase passed its bar, Ideas need picking, findings need approving, or the Story is **drained** and the crew has run out of work it is authorised to do — and **stalled**, the only flavour that means trouble: the loop stopped converging and hit a wall. Both are normal; neither is an error (§6.6, L-34, L-37, L-37a). A loop that quietly spins for three days on a contradiction only a human can see is worse than one that stops after an hour and asks.

**P15 — This is a build contract for the first build, not a constitution.**
The engine is expected to change. The design is settled enough to build (§15) and deliberately not settled enough to be final: the Author's own position is _"we build it out, test it in building, and then revise the engine."_ Two consequences for the build: the loop, the store, and the tool surface are built so that a phase model, a role set, or a criterion can be **replaced without rewriting the store**, and anything the spec marks as deferred (recipes, sequel access, §14) stays a **named hole** rather than being quietly designed in. A section that survives contact with a real book unchanged is the surprise, not the norm.

## 3. Glossary

### 3.1 The actors — Author, employee, agent, thread

These words are not synonyms, and using one for another is the wording defect this section exists to prevent:

- **Author** — the human user of the product, and the only terminal authority in it. The Author owns the Seed, the Goal, the locks, every approval, and the decision to stop or complete a Story. The Author is never an employee and never an agent.
- **Employee** — a standing member of the global roster: **Writer**, **Editor**, **Reader**, **Checker**, **Researcher**. An employee has a kind, a rank, a model, and a §7 permission row. It is configured once, exists across every Story, and does nothing on its own.
- **Agent** — an employee **while a thread is executing**. "Agent" is an execution noun: it names the runtime actor inside `@underload/harness`, never a tile, a roster entry, or a template role. An idle employee is not an agent; an agent exists only for the life of its thread.
- **Thread** — one execution of one employee for one Work Item: one Run, one brief, one fresh context. A thread lives inside the Story phase's **session** and is destroyed when the Run reports. The Run row is the bookkeeping record; the thread is the engine object; they are one-to-one.
- **Phrase** — two things wear this word, and the split is deliberate. (a) The UI's name for a Story phase's **session** (P1 Summary, P2 Plotlines, P3 Chapters): threads run inside a phrase; a Run is a thread, never a phrase. (b) The **Summary Phrase**: the §4.19 record kind (`phrases.key = 'summary'`) that holds the composed Overview the five Phrase Groups produce. The rail shows exactly one phrase of sense (a) — Summary — and inside it the Author edits groups, not phrases. Nothing in the store, the loop or the data model branches on sense (a).
- **Phrase Group** — one of the five named, separately revisable parts of the Summary Phrase: **Premise**, **Promise**, **Theme**, **Tone**, **Ending** (`shared/phrase.ts`, §4.19). A group is a row (`phrase_groups`), it has its own revision line, and it is the unit of a Writer install, a live hold and a finding's target.
- **Group Revision** — one text of one group, numbered from that group's own line (§4.19, R2). Exactly one group revision is `latest`; the ones it replaces are kept as `archived`. A group the batch did not write gets no revision row and keeps both its text and its number.
- **Overview** — the composed Summary text: the five groups' latest texts, in catalog order, under `## <title>` headings (§4.19). It is generated, never hand-edited, and it is what phase ②/③ and the deep review read.
- **Template** — a crew definition: roles, prompts, `selection_rules`, `attempts`, done-definition. A template role says _which employee kind_ takes a job and _how_; the template is neither an employee nor an agent.
- **Rank** — the harness engine's delegation authority, `A` highest. Delegation is strictly downward, so same-rank peers cannot delegate to one another. **Only a Rank A employee may own a dispatched Work Item, take action on a phase's requirements, and report to the loop.** A Rank B employee MAY be added to a harness, but only as a delegation target inside a Rank A thread: it never receives a Work Item, never gets a Run row of its own, and never reports to the loop independently. Rank is not a quality grade, never gates a tool (T-6a), and never changes a §7 permission row.

**V-1** The meanings above are the only ones used in this document. Roster language says **employee**; execution language says **agent**; the unit of execution is a **thread**; the phase's session is a **phrase**. The **Editor** is the Author's one channel: a directive is addressed to the Editor, and the Editor's review turns it into queued tasks. "Entry" is not a roster concept and is never shown to the Author; the harness's internal session-routing name is the Editor.
**V-2** The loop dispatches Work Items only to Rank A employees. A Rank B employee is legal only inside a Rank A thread's delegation, and that thread remains the one that acts and reports.
**V-3** (Summary Phrase, 2026-11-24.) The phrase/session word and the Summary-Phrase word are not interchangeable: a document, a comment or a screen that means the writable parts of the Summary says **group**, **group revision**, **Overview** or **group vector**, and never a bare "phrase". A group is addressed in code and on the wire by its `phrase_groups.id` (a UUID); the catalog key (`premise`, `promise`, `theme`, `tone`, `ending`) is for humans and prompts. "World rules" is neither a group nor a revision line: it is §4.2's deprecated field, and no writer, reader or install may mint a group for it (R1).

## 4. Data model

The store is the Dashboard's, not the harness's. Collections below are normative in name and field; a build MAY add fields, MUST NOT rename or drop one.

**D-2** The Dashboard MUST support any number of Stories in any phase, simultaneously, and dispatch work across all of them.

**D-3** While `phase = summary` and `summary_locked` is false, `summaries` is writable by the **Writer** — expanding the Seed is Phase 1's whole job. In the shipped group model the five prose fields are not written by any agent at all: they are the **mirror** of the group install (§4.19, R6), and the hand that moves them is the engine's commit (R5). In the legacy `'sampling'` build the expansion is a tool-less sample the engine installs in the selection commit (§16.6). The record is the Story's source prose either way. It is also writable by the Author at any time, and by an **assistant run the Author explicitly invoked on the Summary field only** — with the one exception R5 draws: on a grouped Story the Author's Summary verb is the per-group `POST /stories/:id/phrases/groups/:groupId/revise`, and the whole-Summary verbs answer 409. Once `summary_locked` is true, every agent write is refused with a message naming P5, and the only way to change it is an Author backtrack (D-25). A locked Summary also makes the install skip the mirror write rather than fail the install (`phrase/revisions.ts`, R6).
**D-3b** Two things are source of truth, and they are different. `stories.idea` is **Author truth**: immutable to every agent at every phase, never proposed, never inferred, never drifted. `summaries.chapter_count` is **Author truth too**: declared by the Author, never chosen by the Writer, and a hard fact — Phase 2 produces exactly that many plotlines and a deviation is a failing criterion (C-0), not a creative choice. The Summary's _prose_ is the **Phase 1 artifact**: it is negotiated until the lock and law after it.
**D-3d** The Summary is never accepted as its first draft, and phase ① reaches that conclusion without the chapter machinery in either of its two readings. On a **grouped** Summary (R2/R5, §17) the discipline is not "N versions of everything, keep one" but **"the parts a defect named, rewritten by name"**: a batch is refused unless every group in it is answered (`parseGroupDraftAnswer`), the accepted text installs and is immediately read by `summary_readers` independent seats, a group gets two rewrite attempts per text, and nothing that was written is ever deleted. On a **legacy** Summary the divergence is **sampling**: N tool-less one-turn Writer samples per round, M tool-less screening passes per sample, and an engine selection that is deterministic — fewest `critical` → fewest `high` → fewest total → the earliest sample — with the losers hard-deleted (§16.1, §16.6). Phases ② and ③ keep §6.2a's divergence-and-selection shape (N Writer expansions, checkers, an Editor applying `selection_rules`, losers deleted under D-22), and the principle is unchanged: the Seed is short and the thing the whole book hangs off is not a place to accept the first draft. An Editor's phase-① choice existed in the 2026-11-22 build and was removed by the research pipeline (§16.8).
**D-3c** A change to `chapter_count` after the plotlines lock MUST reopen Phase 2 by backtrack (D-25): added chapters enter the queue as `outlined`, removed chapters are deleted with their prose held for the Author's decision, and every dependent chapter is marked `needs_recheck`.
**D-4** The locked Summary is the root contract. Every Plotline MUST be traceable to it; the loop's first consistency check is Plotline-vs-Summary, before any chapter exists. Above the Summary sits the Seed, which the Summary itself must be traceable to (C-14) — the chain is `idea → summary → plotlines → chapters`, and every artifact names the one above it.
**D-5** Changing an **unlocked** Summary MUST mark every dependent Plotline and Chapter `needs_recheck` (§6.5). A _locked_ Summary cannot be changed by an agent at all; the Author changes it by backtracking, which scraps the dependents rather than rechecking them (L-11, D-25).

**D-11** Tasks are the loop's work queue and are agent-writable: a reviewer that finds a defect files the task that fixes it. This is how the goal is reachable "regardless of number of turns" — the queue, not the transcript, holds the plan.

### 4.8 `digests` — one current record per Story, plus history

`story_id`, `rev`, `body` (bible recap + per-chapter one-line summary + open threads + cast state + last change notes), `stale`.

**D-12** The Digest is rebuilt or patched on every commit that changes story state.
**D-12a** **The Digest is derived, not authored.** The **loop engine** rebuilds it deterministically from the committed change history — never a crew, never the Writer (P10 excepted here deliberately). The reason is that the Digest is the one artifact every fresh run trusts without checking: if the same agent that wrote chapter 9 also summarised it, a wrong chapter 9 becomes a wrong bible and the error is invisible downstream forever. So: the engine composes it from commits and the records they touched; a crew may only file `digest_gap` (D-13) to say the derivation is insufficient; and because it is a pure function of committed history, **any past Digest is reconstructible** — the Author can see what a reviewer was actually told at the time it judged chapter 20. Its `rev` advances with the commits it summarises, and it carries no `why` because no one chose it.
**D-13** A run's brief is: `Digest + the artifact under review + the criteria`. A run MUST NOT be required to read the whole book; if the Digest is insufficient for a judgment, the run files a `digest_gap` finding and the loop repairs the Digest.
**D-14** A stale Digest blocks a review, not a fix. The loop refreshes then re-dispatches.

### 4.9 `verdicts`

`story_id`, `target_ref`, `target_kind`, `run_ref`, `template_ref`, `round`, `criteria_passed` (list of `{criterion, result, evidence, finding_ref}`), `hard_pass`, `soft_count`, `recommendation` (`advance`, `fix`, `stuck` — stuck means the checker believes the Story should go `pending`).

**D-15** A verdict with no `evidence` string on a failing criterion is invalid and MUST be rejected by the tool.

### 4.10 `findings`

`story_id`, `target_ref`, `criterion_ref`, `title`, `detail`, `evidence`, `cost` (`critical`, `major`, `minor`, `taste`), `fingerprint`, `state` (`unconfirmed`, `confirmed`, `approved`, `fixed`, `disputed`, `discarded`), `confirmed_by` (an Editor run, or empty), `approved_by` (the Author, or `auto` — D-35), `seen_count`, **`group_refs`** (additive, Summary Phrase R3: the `phrase_groups.id` UUIDs this defect is about).

**R3 — a finding's groups (2026-11-24).** On a grouped Story a finding against the Summary MUST carry `group_refs`, and the tool refuses one that does not (`tools/checker.ts`, refusal "R3") — as it refuses a ref naming a group the book does not have. The reason is cost, not tidiness: a complaint with no group means "the whole Overview", and the next Writer pass therefore rewrites all five (R2's whole point lost). The rule and its one exemption:

- **absent / empty `group_refs` = every group**, the legacy whole-Overview reading, and `phrase/findings.ts:findingGroupScope` spells the expansion out rather than hiding it. That reading is what keeps a pre-redesign finding meaningful on a book that has moved to groups. It is NOT available to a new finding on a grouped book: `raiseFinding` refuses a phase-① finding on the Summary that names no group (refusal `R3`, listing `key=id` for each group), so the crew cannot file a defect the planner can only route to all five. A chapter or plotline finding, and a legacy book's Summary finding, are untouched by the rule;
- **an id the Story does not have is refused, never dropped** (same `R3` guard, before the D-16 dedupe), because silently dropping the only ref would silently widen the finding to "the whole Overview" — the most expensive reading there is here. The `fix` that comes back with it BRANCHES by book (hardening, D-61): a legacy Story is told it may omit `group_refs`, a grouped Story is told to name every group the defect crosses, because on that book omission is refused one rule later and there is no unscoped Summary to omit into;
- **a repeat sighting widens the scope** rather than filing a second claim: D-16's dedupe merges `group_refs` the same way it merges `related_refs`, so a second Reader naming one more group broadens the row the loop is already working on;
- **closure is per-scope and all-or-nothing.** `findingResolution` reports `{targeted, resolved, missing, resolved_all}`, where a group "resolves" the finding only by the explicit claim on the row — a `group_revisions` row whose `finding_refs` names the finding. `closeFindingWhenGroupsResolved` flips the finding to `fixed` only when `resolved_all` AND the row is `approved` (D-33/D-34: an unapproved row must not close over the Author's gate), and it is idempotent, so a replayed install changes nothing. There is no "partly fixed" state in this ladder; the open finding with its `missing` groups is the honest answer, and those groups are what the next batch is built from;
- **a multi-group finding buys ONE pass, not one per group.** `loop/work.ts:groupWorkTargets` unions every still-owed group of the batch, so a defect naming Premise and Ending is answered by one `draft_groups` run over both, and the install stamps `finding_refs` on both new revisions.

**Open** means any of `unconfirmed`, `confirmed`, `approved` that is not yet `fixed` — the shorthand used throughout §6 and §10.

**D-33** A finding raised by a **Reader or checker** starts `unconfirmed`: it is a suspicion, and suspicions are wrong often enough to be worth checking before they cost a Writer a pass. An **Editor** verifies it against the artifact and either confirms it — which makes it eligible for a task and for the loop's progress accounting — or disputes it. A `critical` finding may open a task immediately, but it still needs the Editor's confirmation before it may _invalidate_ anything or move the Story toward `pending`.
**D-34** **Nothing reaches the Writer without the Author's approval.** The chain is fixed: a Reader or checker **raises** (with a cost), an **Editor verifies and rewrites** the finding into something the Author can read in one pass — the claim, the evidence line, the cost it actually deserves, not the reviewer's raw prose — and the **Author approves** it, which is what creates the task. An unapproved finding generates no Writer work, however confident the reviewer sounded. This is the gate the Author asked for, and it is why the loop can run continuously without running ahead of them.
**D-35** Because the Author "will almost certainly approve" the serious ones, the Story carries an **auto-approve switch**: a per-cost dial (`critical`, `major`, `minor`, `taste`) listing which costs skip the approval wait and go straight to a task. It defaults to **on for `critical`, `major` and `minor`, off for `taste`**, and it lives beside Stop on the Story view. With it on, phase ③ runs unattended (L-35); with it off, the Author is the throttle and the loop waits for them finding by finding. Turning it off is how the Author takes the wheel without stopping the crew.
**D-35b** The dial does a second job, and this is why it is the most important control on the Story view: **it sets the drain threshold** (L-37). A cost the dial covers is a cost the loop will keep working on until none are left; a cost it does not cover is one the loop will raise, verify, and then set down in front of the Author and stop working on. So the dial answers both "what may start without asking me?" and **"how finished is finished?"** — widen it and the crew polishes further before it calls the book done; narrow it and the loop stops earlier and hands the leftovers over. One knob, one meaning: how much of this book the Author wants automated.
**D-34a** **The Editor's triage is final.** It decides what is `critical`, what is `minor`, and what is `disputed`, and there is no appeal step and no second reviewer above it. This is not an oversight: the Editor exists precisely so the Author is not swarmed by things a fresh reviewer said with confidence and meant loosely. The Author's control is upstream — the template's instructions and `selection_rules` (P12) — and downstream — approving, discarding, and completing (D-34, L-26). An Editor that files the wrong things is a template the Author edits (G-7), not a decision the Author re-makes forty times a day. The reviewer's original `title`, `detail`, and `cost` stay on the record (P11) so the call can be audited afterwards, but nothing in the UI forces that comparison and nothing in the loop waits for one.

**D-16** `fingerprint` = stable hash of (criterion, target, normalised subject). A repeat report increments `seen_count` on the existing finding rather than creating a new one. This is what makes "no progress" detectable in a system where every reviewer is amnesiac (§6.7).

### 4.11 `goals`

`story_id` (or empty = a reusable preset), `title`, `threshold_soft` (integer N), `criteria` (see §5), `flat_rounds`, `regress_tolerance`.

**G-5** There is no token field, no minute field, and no round cap on a Goal. Nothing about a Goal can _stop_ a loop. `flat_rounds` and `regress_tolerance` are **loop-detection** parameters only: they decide when a Story that has stopped converging becomes `pending` for the Author (§6.8). A loop that is still improving never stops, however long it takes — and what does stop one is a sign-off, an approval wait, or **drained** (E-0a), none of which is a field on the Goal.

### 4.12 `templates` — the harness catalogue

`name`, `title`, `description`, `roles` (list of `{name, kind: 'writer' | 'editor' | 'reader' | 'checker' | 'researcher', prompt, model?, web?, agents?, aggregate?}`), `serves_phase`, `done_definition`, `selection_rules`, `attempts`, `delegation`, `escalation_rule`, `next_phase`, `max_concurrent`, `builtin`, `editable`.

**D-38** **A template is a crew definition, not an employee and not an agent.** It declares roles, and a role assigns an **employee kind** to a job. A role may also declare `agents: N` — N fresh agents of that employee kind running inside one run, whose outputs the role **aggregates** to a single answer (`aggregate`: `majority` | `merge` | `lead`). A single Editor template may therefore contain three editor agents reading the same three drafts and return one selection. The aggregation is **internal to the run**: the loop still receives exactly one report, one `selection_note`, one verdict set, and one permission-bearing answer (S-1, D-17e). This is how "one Editor or a panel?" is answered without a vote anywhere in the loop — the panel lives inside the harness, so the Author's taste is still applied **once per decision**, and D-34a (the Editor's call is final) stays true. What a run MUST surface when its internals disagreed: the `selection_note` or the finding's `detail` names the dissent and the split ("2 of 3 judged this `critical`; confirmed at `major`"), because a hidden disagreement inside an authoritative answer is the one thing the Author cannot tune.
**D-38a** **The Author chooses how many roles each crew has, and how many threads run.** Templates are the unit the Author assigns, and there is no fixed count of Writer, Editor, or Reader roles in the design — `max_concurrent` and `attempts` are the Author's settings, not engine constants. The one structural rule is per-unit, not per-Story: a unit with more than one `competing` revision waits for a selection (D-23). Assigning a second Writer _template_ to a Story is legal and means two voices; the loop then reports style divergence between them as an ordinary `taste` finding rather than treating it as an error — the Author asked for it. Every built-in employee holds Rank A; a Rank B employee may be added, but only as a delegation target (V-2).

**D-17a** `roles[].kind` names **which employee kind takes the job**, and selects **the brief and the permission row**, never the tool list (T-0, T-6a): a `writer` employee may write story artifacts, an `editor` may select, merge, and verify, a `reader` may propose Ideas and rate, a `checker` may report. All four see all tools. A kind with no instructions is the actual failure mode: an agent holding `editChapter` and no rule about it will use it.
**D-17b** Write permission comes **only from a template role of the Writer employee kind assigned to the Story** — no other kind may write a story artifact, however capable its tools. How many Writer roles are assigned is the Author's choice (D-38a): one is the default and the simplest case for P10's single voice, two is legal and means two voices, and `serves_phase` is how a structural Writer for ① and ② coexists with a prose Writer for ③. `editor` roles hold the selection and verification permissions and may write **only** through selection, merge, and confirmation (D-33). Duplicating the Writer role is how the Author gets more than one writing voice available.
**D-17c** `selection_rules` is the Editor's brief in the Author's own words: what it prefers, what it weighs, what it refuses, and how it merges. It is the mechanism by which P12 becomes executable — an Editor with no `selection_rules` is guessing at the Author's taste.
**D-17d** `attempts` is how many parallel revisions the loop spawns for one unit (1, 3, 5…). With no cost constraint it defaults to **more than one**, because divergence is the whole advantage of a fresh-thread architecture.
**D-17e** `delegation` names the roles this template may delegate to _inside its own run_ (`@underload/harness` subagent delegation). A single Editor run may therefore fan out to its own fresh sub-contexts to reach a conclusion before it commits to one revision. Delegation stays inside one run and one Story: the delegated sub-context is an **agent** of that role's employee kind, not a new employee or Work Item, and the delegating thread remains the one that acts and reports (V-2).
**D-17** The Author MUST be able to create a template from scratch and **duplicate an existing one**, then edit roles, prompts, `agents` and `aggregate` (D-38), `selection_rules`, `attempts`, and done-definition. Built-ins are duplicated, never edited in place.

### 4.13 `runs`

`story_id`, `work_item_ref`, `template_ref`, `role`, `session_ref`, `state` (`spawning`, `working`, `reporting`, `torn_down`, `failed`, `abandoned`), `started_at`, `ended_at`, `tokens`, `brief_hash`, **`group_refs`** (additive, Summary Phrase R4: the `phrase_groups.id` UUIDs this run is writing — copied onto the row by `run/executor.ts` from the Work Item, and the ONE reading of "who holds this group" that both the exclusion and the retry allowance use).

**R4 — the live hold is a fact about rows, not a semaphore (2026-11-24).** `phrase/exclusion.ts` derives it from the live `runs` rows whose `work_item_ref` starts with a HOLDING family (`GROUP_HOLDING_KINDS = ['draft_groups']` — a `review_groups` or deep-review run carries refs for all five groups and holds none of them). Three consequences are normative:
- **fail closed.** A live holding run with no `group_refs` (an old row, or a skipped copy step) holds EVERY group of the Story: one deferred sweep is cheaper than two writers installing over each other and an archived revision nobody reviewed.
- **disjoint batches run concurrently; an overlapping batch is SHRUNK, not cancelled.** `partitionGroupRefs` returns `{free, held}`, so `{premise, tone}` with premise already live still offers `{tone}`. Cancelling the whole batch would make the exclusion a serialiser and idle the queue while one group's writer works. A batch that is entirely held offers nothing — and no claim key is spent on an empty answer.
- **reading is not writing.** A group's two rewrite attempts (`GROUP_WRITE_ATTEMPT_CAP`) count only closed HOLDING runs whose journal position is after the write that produced that group's CURRENT text (`exclusion.ts:writeBaselineOf`), so a live run is not counted, N reading seats never spend the allowance to rewrite what they read, and the pair is a bound on re-drafting ONE text — an install moves the baseline and re-arms the pair against the new text, and a Seed reset re-baselines with it (§17.6).

**D-18** `runs` is the live crew panel's data source. A run row exists from thread spawn to teardown so the Author can watch the crew and pull the cord on a specific thread. The row is bookkeeping; the employee becomes an agent only for the thread it records.

### 4.14 `directives`

`story_id`, `text`, `state` (`queued`, `propagating`, `done`, `rejected`), `impact` (the list of plotlines/chapters/ledger entries the loop determined it must touch).

**D-19** A directive is not a chat message. It is a decision the Author addresses to the **Editor**, and the loop must propagate it. The server records the directive, resolves the artifacts it names, marks dependents `needs_recheck`, stops runs writing into a changed artifact, and reports what it touched; a dispatched `directive_work` review then runs as a fresh Editor thread and opens the tasks the direction implies with `createTask`. The sweep reads the queue and opens a fresh thread per task — this is not delegation: the Editor queues Work Items, and no nested conversation stands in for them. If no Editor review produces tasks, the deterministic fallback queues one task per resolved artifact so the direction is never dropped.

### 4.15 `revisions` — competing attempts at one unit

`story_id`, `unit_kind` (`summary` | `plotline` | `chapter`), `unit_ref`, `body`, `run_ref`, `template_ref`, `brief_hash`, `state` (`pending`, `drafted`, `competing`, `selected`, `merged_into`, `discarded`), `verdict_refs`, `selection_note`. The LEGACY phase ① sampler adds four optional fields: `origin` (`sampled` in round 1, `refined` from round 2), `round` (1-based), `prompt_hash` (the byte-stable hash of the compiled sampling prompt that produced the row, §16.5), and `issue_counts` (`{critical, high}` totals written by the selection, §16.6). A sample is filed `drafted` by the engine in the commit that closes its run; it is never `pending`. **A grouped Summary writes nothing here** — its texts are §4.19's `group_revisions` rows — so on a `'groups'` book the `unit_kind: 'summary'` slice of this collection is the pre-import audit only, which is exactly what `GET /stories/:id`'s `sampling` projection reads (§17).

**A draft is `pending` while its Writer is still working on it, and `drafted` once that Writer's run returns.** A later write in the same run extends the same `pending` row rather than filing a second, partial draft. When the run closes, the server checks the draft: a Summary must carry every prose block (§4.2 — `premise`, `promise`, `theme`, `tone`, `ending`, `world_rules`), and only then is it `drafted` for review. A Summary that closes short of a block stays `pending` and is handed back to its Writer with the missing blocks named. Readers, Checkers and the Editor read `drafted` drafts and never a `pending` one; `competing` is the historical spelling of a reviewable draft and is read as `drafted`.

**D-21** Revisions of one unit are visible **side by side** to an Editor and to the Author, and to nobody else: a Writer's brief names the unit and its contract, never its sibling drafts (P2, P13).
**D-22** When an Editor selects or merges — or, in the LEGACY phase ① sampler, when the engine selects (§16.6) — the server MUST delete every non-selected revision of that unit in the same commit. Deletion is hard, not a flag — a discarded draft that lingers is context rot with a badge on it. The `selection_note` and the verdicts survive; the prose does not. **The group model does not use this collection for the Summary at all, and so never deletes a Summary text: R2 supersedes the deletion here** (§4.19 — an install archives the row it replaces, and history is read, not truncated).
**D-23** A unit with more than one `competing` revision is not eligible for the next phase. The gate is the Editor's selection, not the passage of time or a verdict count.
**D-24** The Author may always override an Editor: select a loser, restore a discarded revision from the run's own output, or write the unit by hand. The Editor's choice is the Author's choice, automated — not a verdict above the Author.
**D-25** **Backtrack is the only way a locked phase opens, and it is Author-only.** Backing up to phase N unlocks phase N and **deletes everything later phases produced** — the prose and the structure, hard, by the same rule that deletes a losing revision (D-22). The reopened phase starts from the locked artifact above it with no memory of the scrapped pass; the verdicts, findings, and `selection_note`s of that pass survive as provenance (P11) — the Ideas do not, because the lock that closed that phase already deleted them (D-36). The alternative — keeping chapters written against a plotline the Author has since rejected — is P2's context rot at book scale, and it is why the phases lock at all. One consequence worth naming: a phase that was locked has already thrown away its Ideas (D-36), so a backtrack reopens it with an **empty tray**, and the Readers may well propose again what the Author rejected last time. That is the price of a store that stays small, and the Author pays it once per backtrack rather than every run.
**D-25a** **Editing the Seed is a backtrack to phase ①, in full.** The Author's idea is the root of the whole chain (D-4), so changing it invalidates everything grown from it: the Summary, every plotline, every chapter, and every digest go the same way D-25 sends the artifacts of a scrapped phase. There is no partial version of this and no "recheck" — a new idea is a new book wearing the same title. The confirmation names that plainly, because it is the most destructive action in the product. **Spinning a sibling Story from the same idea is a manual copy, not a feature**: the Author copies the Seed text into a new Story. The product does not fork Stories, keep divergent lineages, or offer an "duplicate with history" option — one Story row, one book, and the Seed is plain text that is trivial to copy.

### 4.17 `ideas` — what Readers and the Author put forward, before anything is written

**Phase-scoped and temporary.** An Idea lives only inside the phase that raised it. Locking a phase deletes that phase's Ideas — selected, discarded, deferred alike (D-36). What persists is the artifact they produced, not the argument that produced it.

`story_id`, `phase`, `unit_ref` (empty = whole-Story), `body`, `kind` (`direction` | `possibility` | `fact` | `warning`), `author` (a Reader role or the Author), `sources` (list of `{url_or_title, claim, accessed_at}`), `fingerprint`, `seen_count`, `state` (`proposed` | `verified` | `refuted` | `unverifiable` | `selected` | `discarded` | `deferred`), `verification` (`{verdict, evidence, meets_guidelines, checked_by_run, checked_at}`), `selection_note`, `absorbed_into` (the artifact that took it, once written).

**D-31** The Editor checks every Idea against the **Author's guidelines** — the Goal criteria, the `tone` sheet, `world_rules`, and its own `selection_rules` — and records `meets_guidelines` alongside the fact-check. An Idea can be true and still unwanted; both answers are the Editor's job (D-27).

**D-26** An Idea is a **proposal, never an edit**. A Reader may not change a Summary sentence, a plotline, or a chapter; it files an Idea and the Writer is the only thing that later writes what the Author selected (P10).
**D-27** An Idea carrying `sources` MUST be verified by an **Editor before it can be selected** (C-15). A Reader's web result is a lead, not a fact: the Editor re-reads the source, or finds a second one, and records `verdict` with evidence. The Author selects among _verified_ Ideas first and sees unverified ones marked as such.
**D-28** Ideas are cheap and expected to be wrong: `attempts` on a Reader role controls how many run per unit, and a phase with zero Ideas filed is reported as a **thin crew**, not a clean one. **Within its phase, a discarded Idea is never deleted** — unlike a losing revision (D-22), because a discarded draft is a dead alternative while a discarded Idea is a _decision_. The Editor and the Author both need to remember it, or the next fresh Reader proposes it again and the loop argues the same point forever. **Across a phase boundary it is deleted** (D-36): the decision did its work, the artifact carries it, and the argument is not context the next phase needs.
**D-32** Discarded and deferred Ideas are **the memory that prevents the loop** (P14) — and they are exactly as long-lived as the loop they prevent, which is one phase. Every Reader and Editor brief carries the phase's decided Ideas — `selected`, `discarded`, `deferred`, with their `selection_note`s — so an amnesiac crew cannot re-litigate a settled question. A new Idea whose `fingerprint` matches a decided one MUST be reported to the Author as a **repeat** with the earlier decision attached, not silently re-opened; a Reader that keeps re-proposing what the Author has rejected three times is a template the Author edits (G-7).
**D-36** **Locking a phase deletes that phase's Ideas.** The lock is the moment the argument becomes an artifact, so the tray is emptied in the same commit: `selected` Ideas have already been absorbed (D-29), and `discarded`, `deferred`, `refuted`, and `unverifiable` ones are deleted outright. The phase's `verdicts`, `findings`, and `selection_note`s survive as provenance (P11) — the Author must still be able to ask "why this Summary?" — but the pile of maybes does not cross into the next phase. This is what keeps a long Story's brief from filling with decisions nobody can act on any more, and it is why the memory in D-32 is scoped to a phase rather than to a Story.
**D-29** The Author's selection of an Idea is a **recorded decision**, not a chat message (P3): `state: selected` plus `selection_note`, and the Writer's brief for the next run names the selected Ideas it must absorb.

### 4.16 `proposals`

`story_id`, `target_ref`, `target_field`, `patch`, `why`, `run_ref`, `state` (`pending`, `accepted`, `rejected`). **D-20** Every P9 propose-only edit lands here, never in the target field.
**D-20a** This collection is the **agent→Author** direction only. The Author's own edit of chapter prose is a direct write, not a proposal — there is no one to approve it (D-37). An Author edit is attributed like any other mutation (P11, `run_ref` null, `author: true`) and is undoable.

### 4.18 phase ①'s three collections — `knowledge`, `seed_briefs`, `sample_reviews`

These are APPENDED to §4, never inserted: the seventeen collections above keep their names and their order, and an old snapshot loads with these three empty (§16.2 has the full field tables).

- **`knowledge`** — the Researcher's artifact (§16.2, §16.3): `story_id`, optional `phase`, `kind`, `topic`, `claim`, optional `detail`, `importance` (`critical`/`high`/`normal`/`low`), `sources` (each `{url_or_title, claim, accessed_at}`, T-10), `fingerprint` (D-16 dedupe key), `seen_count`, `author`.
- **`seed_briefs`** — the Editor's derived projection of the Seed (§16.2): `story_id`, `seed_hash`, `logline`, `protagonist`, `want`, optional `need`, `obstacle`, `stakes`, `setting`, `world_rules[]`, `promise`, optional `tone_hints`, `constraints[]`, `open_questions[]`, `status` (`draft`/`accepted`), optional `notes`.
- **`sample_reviews`** — one screening Reader's one-turn answer about one sample (§16.2, §16.5): `story_id`, `revision_ref`, `reader`, `issues[]` (each `{severity: 'critical'|'high', criterion, location, quote, detail, fix_hint?}`), `valid`. These are **not** findings: they never enter D-33/D-34 and cannot block a Writer; they rank the round's samples and seed the deep review. On a grouped Summary the same collection also carries the `GroupReview` seats' answers (§17), and `SampledIssue` gained an optional `group_refs` so one wire shape covers both — a legacy row simply carries none.

### 4.19 The Summary Phrase collections — `phrases`, `phrase_groups`, `group_revisions` (2026-11-24, R1/R2/R6)

Appended to §4 exactly as §4.18's three were: nothing above is renamed, no snapshot is migrated, and an old book simply has these three collections empty until the Author asks for rows (R6). The catalog itself — which groups exist, what they are called, what each owes, and the order they compose in — is **product data in `src/shared/phrase.ts`**, not code shape (P15): a store, a loop or a route that branches on a group name is a defect (R1).

- **`phrases`** — one row per ENABLED phrase definition; this build ships exactly one, `key: 'summary'` ("Summary Phrase"). `story_id`, `key`, `title`, `description`, `order`, `content` (**the composed Overview — generated, never hand-written**), `revision` (how many group installs this phrase has had; bumped once per install, not once per group), `group_refs` (the group ids in catalog order — the list the projection and the review brief read). A disabled definition is known to the catalog and deliberately NOT created, so a second phrase kind is one flag in the catalog and no loop change.
- **`phrase_groups`** — one row per writable part, holding its CURRENT text. `story_id`, `phrase_id`, `key` (`premise` | `promise` | `theme` | `tone` | `ending`), `title`, `description` (what the group owes, in the group's own words — the text the prompt assembler quotes verbatim), `order`, `content` (`''` = never written), `revision` (the latest revision's number, `0` = never written), `updated_by` (the run that installed the current text, P11). The **id is a persisted UUID**, and it is the group's ADDRESS: the revise route, a finding's `group_refs`, a run's `group_refs` and a revision's `group_id` all name it, so the R4 hold and the R3 closure are provable from the book after a restart. There is deliberately no `state` field here: latest/archived belongs to the revision row, and a second copy is a field that can disagree with it.
- **`group_revisions`** — one row per accepted text of one group, `number` 1-based and sequential **within that group**. `story_id`, `phrase_id`, `group_id` (the UUID), `group_key` (denormalised so history survives a catalog retitle), `number`, `content` (the group's text verbatim), `state` (`latest` | `archived` — exactly one `latest` per group), `run_ref` (null for a legacy import's seed row), `work_item_ref`, `task_refs` (the open fix tasks this text was written to answer, frozen at dispatch so a late task cannot claim an old revision), `finding_refs` (**R3's closure anchor**), `knowledge_used`, `prompt_hash` (the compiler's byte-stable 16-char digest — §5's cache rule, P11).

**R1 — the catalog is data.** Five groups, in reading order: what the story IS, what it PROMISES, what it is ABOUT, how it SOUNDS, where it LANDS. Adding, retitling or retiring a group is an edit to `shared/phrase.ts`. `world_rules` gets no row (R1), and `ensurePhraseCatalog` is idempotent — a route that creates a Story twice over a restored store adds nothing.

**R2 — independence and retention.** An install writes one new `latest` per group it actually wrote, archives the row each replaces, and gives an untouched group neither a new number nor a new row. So **Premise at revision 2 beside Promise at revision 1 is the model working**: the batch, not the Story, is the unit of the commit, and one group's work never revises another's. Nothing is deleted — the superseded text is `archived` and stays readable in the book (contrast D-22, which this supersedes for the Summary).

**R6 — the mirror, and no migration.** The install writes §4.2's five prose fields **in the same commit**, from the same texts, through an IDENTITY map (`SUMMARY_MIRROR_FIELDS`: `premise→premise`, …), so the Digest, phases ②/③, `readSummary` and every pre-redesign reader keep reading `summaries` and can never see a new group text beside a stale mirror. `world_rules` is never written by it. If the Summary row is `locked`, the group install STANDS and the mirror waits — the lock is the Author's declaration (L-7a/P5), the projection reports the difference (`review.mirror: {summary_id, in_sync, locked, fields}`), and the install does not fail. A book with `summary_mode` absent is read as `'sampling'` with these three collections empty: that is the migration, which is to say there isn't one, and `POST /stories/:id/phrases/import` is the Author's explicit, idempotent opt-in that lifts the five legacy prose fields into first revisions (the seeded rows carry `run_ref: null`).

**The composition, stated so it can be asserted exactly** (`shared/phrase.ts:composePhraseContent`): one `## <title>` heading per group with non-empty text, the text under it, entries separated by a single blank line, in `order` rank order, **no trailing newline**. An unwritten group is SKIPPED rather than rendered as an empty heading — an Overview of empty headings reads like a form, not like the thing the deep review judges. A key the catalog does not know sorts LAST instead of vanishing, so a retired group's text is still readable.

**The wire shape** (`GET /stories/:id/phrases`, `api/projections.ts:PhraseView`) is one read for the whole surface, and it never writes: `mode` (the Story's setting echoed, not derived), the phrase's `revision`, the composed `overview`, `groups[]` (`id`, `key`, `title`, `description`, `order`, `content`, `revision`, `updated_by`, `history[]` newest-first **with `chars`, not prose**, `owed_by_findings`, `writer_busy`), `findings[]` (with `group_refs`, `unscoped`, `targeted`/`resolved`/`missing`/`resolved_all`), `tasks[]`, and `review` (`unwritten_groups`, `written_groups`, `vector`, `vector_hash`, `review_seats`, `reviews_on_current_vector`, `deep_reviews_on_current_vector`, `open_findings`, `blocking_findings`, `open_tasks`, `mirror`). **U3's rule: the rail shows the latest text and the COUNT of superseded revisions, because the archived bodies are not on this read** — a row a reader cannot open is decoration, and a rail that lists them is a promise the wire does not keep.

**The desk reads this and nothing else** (§10.2's U-18/U-19): a group's revision number belongs in its own pane, not in the rail; a disabled Revise control and the route's 409 come from the same `writer_busy` read so a button and a refusal cannot disagree; and a Summary the group rows call incomplete is never presented as sign-off-ready (`story-contract.ts:groupSignOffBlockers`), even though the HTTP lock gate stays exactly as §6.3a and §10.2/U-16 describe it.

## 5. The Goal

### 5.1 Shape

A Goal is an ordered list of **criteria**, each:

```ts
type Criterion = {
    id: string;
    label: string; // shown to the Author, plain language
    question: string; // the exact question a reviewer answers
    checker: 'code' | 'judge'; // mechanical vs. LLM
    hard: boolean; // hard = must pass; soft = counts toward threshold
    fix_hint: string; // the smallest change that would satisfy it
};
```

A Story passes when **all hard criteria pass** and **soft passes ≥ `threshold_soft`**.

### 5.2 The built-in criteria

| #    | criterion                                                                                                                                      | checker | hard |
| ---- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------- | ---- |
| C-0  | the Story has exactly `chapter_count` plotlines and chapters — no more, no fewer                                                               | code    | yes  |
| C-1  | every chapter has a plotline                                                                                                                   | code    | yes  |
| C-2  | every plotline traces to the Summary — nothing in it contradicts premise, promise, theme, or ending                                            | judge   | yes  |
| C-3  | every plotline's `must_change` is actually a change, not a restatement                                                                         | judge   | no   |
| C-4  | plotlines are consistent with each other: no contradiction between adjacent chapters, no event requiring a setup that no earlier chapter makes | judge   | yes  |
| C-5  | every `setup` in the ledger has a `payoff`, and every `payoff` has a setup                                                                     | code    | yes  |
| C-6  | timeline is monotonic; no `fact` contradiction                                                                                                 | code    | yes  |
| C-7  | expanded prose delivers its chapter's `must_happen`                                                                                            | judge   | yes  |
| C-8  | expanded prose delivers its chapter's `must_change`                                                                                            | judge   | yes  |
| C-9  | no character acts against `arc` + `state_now` without a scene that owns the change                                                             | judge   | no   |
| C-10 | the ending pays the `promise`                                                                                                                  | judge   | yes  |
| C-11 | no two consecutive chapters do the same job (pacing)                                                                                           | judge   | no   |
| C-12 | prose quality at the level the `tone` sheet demands                                                                                            | judge   | no   |
| C-13 | the prose reads as though this Author wrote it — matches the `tone` sheet and the Editor templates' `selection_rules`                          | judge   | yes  |
| C-14 | the Summary delivers the Seed: nothing in it contradicts what the Seed states, and no commitment the Seed states explicitly goes unpaid        | judge   | yes  |
| C-15 | every Idea that reached a locked artifact was verified by an Editor and carries evidence                                                       | code    | yes  |

The criteria group into **one level per phase boundary, plus a whole-Story level**, and the loop reports them separately because they fail for different reasons:

| level                    | phase | criteria                          | question                                                                                |
| ------------------------ | ----- | --------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Seed → Summary**       | 1     | C-14, C-15                        | does the Summary deliver the idea the Author actually had?                              |
| **Summary → Plotlines**  | 2     | C-0, C-1, C-2, C-3, C-4, C-5, C-6 | do the plotlines serve the story the Author declared, in the count the Author declared? |
| **Plotlines → Chapters** | 3     | C-7, C-8, C-9                     | does each chapter deliver the contract written for it?                                  |
| **Whole Story**          | 3     | C-10, C-11, C-13                  | does the book pay its promise, hold its pace, and sound like the Author?                |

The first level is the one the Author did not name and Phase 1 requires: if the Summary quietly drifts from the Seed, every later level can pass while the book is wrong. C-12 sits outside the levels as a per-chapter quality check.

**G-1** The Author MUST be able to toggle a criterion's `hard` flag, delete one, add one, and change `threshold_soft`, per Story and per chapter.
**G-2** The loop MUST report progress as `"13 of 16 · failing: C-4, C-5, C-11"`, **per phase**, and MUST NOT show a phase's criteria as failing before that phase is reached with each failure naming its `fix_hint`. A bare percentage is a defect.
**G-3** `code` criteria MUST be evaluated by the server without a model call, and MUST be re-evaluated automatically after every commit.
**G-3a** C-13 is the criterion the whole product is measured by, and it is the one no built-in can get right: it asks whether the prose reads as the Author's own. Its evidence is the templates' `selection_rules` and the Summary's `tone` sheet, and the Author is expected to tune it until it stops failing on prose they like. A system that passes C-13 on everything is too loose; one that fails it on everything is too tight.

### 5.3 Fidelity, not quality

**G-6** No criterion may ask "is this good?" Every criterion is answerable against a stored artifact: the Summary, a plotline, the ledger, a template's `selection_rules`. A criterion whose evidence would be taste rather than citation is a build defect.
**G-7** Where a crew's judgment and the Author's stated preference conflict, **the Author's preference wins, including when it is worse.** A template that keeps filing findings the Author keeps rejecting is a template the Author edits — the loop must surface that pattern (repeated `discarded` findings against the same criterion, D-34) rather than keep arguing.
**G-8** The Goal is never "the best story". It is "consistent at every level, and written like mine".

### 5.4 The Goal is the shared target

**G-4** Every template's brief carries the same Goal. Crews do not each hold their own quality theory. The Goal is the one thing that makes many independent fresh reviewers produce a coherent result.

## 6. The loop

### 6.1 The scan

**L-0** The sweep dispatches **only Stories whose `status` is `ready`** (D-2a). A `pending`, `completed`, or `stopped` Story is scanned for reporting but receives no work.
**L-1** The server runs a continuous **sweep** over all Stories. Each sweep:

1. re-evaluates all `code` criteria for every Story;
2. collects open `tasks`, `needs_recheck` markers, **`unconfirmed` findings awaiting an Editor** (D-33), and **`confirmed` findings awaiting the Author's approval** (D-34) — an unverified suspicion is work, and so is an unapproved one. A `confirmed` finding on a Story whose `auto_approve` covers its cost is approved by the sweep itself, on the spot, as `approved_by: auto` (D-35);
3. checks the phase preconditions: is the current phase's Goal passed, is the Story **drained** (L-37), or are the only remaining blockers things only the Author can move? if so, mark `pending` (`phase_signoff` / `idea_selection` / `finding_approval` / `drained`) and **skip dispatch for that Story entirely** (L-29, D-29, L-36);
4. otherwise determines, per Story, the next eligible **Work Item** for the Story's current `phase` — a phase may not be entered unless every earlier phase is locked (L-33);
5. dispatches Work Items to templates that have spare `max_concurrent` capacity, across all Stories, until global concurrency is full;
6. recomputes each Story's `consistency` levels and `trend`, and moves a Story to `pending` (`stalled`) if it has stopped converging (§6.8) — **but only after checking `drained`, which is tested first and wins** (L-37a): a Story that has run out of authorised work is finished-by-standard, not stuck.

**L-2** The sweep MUST be idempotent and MUST use a **claim with lease**: a Work Item claimed by a run cannot be claimed again until the lease expires or the run reports. Two sweeps must never spawn two crews for one job.
**L-3** The sweep MUST survive a restart with no lost state, because all loop state is in the store (P1). On boot it re-adopts or abandons runs whose lease has expired.

### 6.2 The three loops

**Loop S — Summary pass** (phase `summary`). Objective: a Summary that delivers the Seed and is internally logical (C-14, C-15). Whole-Story; it runs until its Goal passes, then stops for the lock (L-29). The shipped loop is the research pipeline of §16.1: the **Editor** structures the Seed into a **Seed Brief**; the **Researcher** gathers sourced **Knowledge**; N tool-less one-turn **Writer** samples expand the brief into competing Summary drafts; M tool-less **Readers** screen each sample; the **engine** selects the least-issue sample deterministically and truncates the rest; the selected revision then gets the tool-using **Reader** deep review, the **Editor's** finding verification and the Story's auto-approve dial, whose approved findings queue the fix tasks that arm the next sampling round. No Editor chooses a phase-① draft any more (D-3d, §16.6), and no Checker runs in phase ①.

**Loop A — Plotline pass** (phase `plotlines`). Objective: every Plotline aligned with the locked Summary and with each other, and exactly `chapter_count` of them (C-0…C-6). Whole-Story, cross-chapter. The **Writer** produces the plotlines by sampling the Summary across `chapter_count` units; Readers propose plotline changes; checkers judge; an **Editor** enforces consistency and chooses between competing plotline drafts; the Author approves.

**Loop B — Chapter pass** (phase `chapters`). Objective: every chapter's prose delivers the plotline bound to it and violates nothing global (C-7…C-9, and C-10/C-11/C-13 at whole-Story level). All chapters in flight at once (L-8), running constantly. This is the **most automated phase in the product**: the Author may watch, stop, or steer, and none of those is required for it to finish (L-35).

The shape of a chapter pass, every sweep:

```
Reader / checker → "this diverges from the plotline", "this reference doesn't hold up",
                   "this spends the mystery too early"        → finding (unconfirmed)
        ↓
Editor → verify against the artifact: confirm or dispute      → finding (open | disputed)
        ↓  confirmed
Writer → fixes it (or an Editor selects between competing drafts)
        ↓
server → commit, propagate to dependent chapters (L-8a), rebuild digest
        ↓
next sweep — the Author watched, or didn't
```

**L-35** Phase ③ is the automated phase, and **the auto-approve switch is what makes it so** (D-35). With `critical` and `major` auto-approved, the chain Reader → Editor → task → Writer runs with the Author watching but touching nothing, and the phase completes on its own. With the switch off, the loop **does** wait on the Author finding by finding — that is the point of turning it off, and it is a throttle, not a stall (D-34). Either way the phase never waits on a _sign-off_: phase ③ ends when it **drains** (L-37), and the Story ends when the Author accepts it (L-26). The Author may stop or steer at any moment and is always honoured (L-10, P8).

**L-4a** Each loop MUST stop at its own sign-off (L-29). Loop S does not "keep improving the Summary" after the Goal passes: it presents, waits, and hands the phase to the Author.

**L-4** The three loops MUST have separate templates and separate done-definitions.
**L-5** Loop S and Loop A MUST NOT run while any chapter is mid-`in_pass`, and vice versa, unless the Author forces it (and neither may run at all while an earlier phase is unlocked — L-33). Two loops editing the same artifact concurrently is a defect.

### 6.2a Divergence and selection

The loop does not produce one draft and then critique it. It produces **several, independently, and then chooses** — this is the shape the architecture exists to enable.

```
Writer run ×N (fresh threads, same brief, same contract, no sight of each other)
        ↓  N competing revisions (§4.15)
Checker runs ×M (fresh threads, read-only) → verdicts + findings per revision
        ↓
Editor run (fresh; may delegate to its own sub-contexts, D-17e)
        ↓  applies the Author's `selection_rules`
select one · merge several · or reject all and re-brief the Writer
        ↓
server deletes every non-selected revision in the same commit (D-22)
        ↓
next sweep
```

**L-16** The N Writer runs for one unit MUST be spawned together and MUST NOT see each other's output (P2). Divergence that knows it is being compared diverges differently.
**L-17** An Editor's decision MUST record a `selection_note` naming the rule it applied and what it preferred in each draft, so the Author can see the taste at work and edit the template when the taste is wrong (P12, G-7).
**L-18** "Reject all and re-brief the Writer" is a first-class outcome, not a failure. An Editor that cannot find a winner is reporting that the brief or the contract is wrong, and that is a finding worth filing.
**L-19** Merging is a Writer operation: an Editor selects the parts and **the Writer writes the merge**, because the Writer owns the prose (P10). An Editor may merge structure; it may not compose sentences.
**L-20** A unit with `attempts > 1` is not eligible for the next phase until an Editor has selected (D-23). The loop may not resolve divergence by picking the first one that passes.

§6.2a is phases ② and ③'s shape, and phase ① is outside it in BOTH of its readings. On a **grouped** Summary (§4.19, §17) there is no competing set at all: one Writer pass writes a batch of groups, its accepted answer installs immediately, and the only verbs are install and archive — `select` and `merge` do not exist for the Summary unit and the routes say so (R5). On a **legacy** `'sampling'` Summary the drafts are samples the engine persists and the engine selects deterministically, and no Editor run chooses between them (§16.1, §16.6). The paragraph above therefore never applies to phase ①.

### 6.3 The gates between phases

**L-6a** Phase 2 is not complete until the Writer has produced exactly `chapter_count` plotlines (C-0). The Writer does not choose the count, and no checker may file a finding that implies a different one — the count is Author truth (D-3b).
**L-6** Each phase transition requires an **explicit Author sign-off**, not merely "the loop reported pass": `summary → plotlines` needs the Summary locked, `plotlines → chapters` needs the plotlines locked. Agents may reach 16/16 and still be waiting on the Author. This is deliberate: expanding an 80%-agreed outline makes every downstream chapter loop burn rounds rediscovering an ambiguity the Author could have settled in one sentence.
**L-7** The Author signs Phase 2 by locking the plotlines: `stories.plotlines_locked` becomes true and every `plotlines[].contract_locked` mirrors it in the same commit. One action on the Dashboard.
**L-7a** The Author signs Phase 1 by locking the Summary: `stories.summary_locked` becomes true. The Author cannot lock a phase while Ideas are still `proposed` — each must be `selected`, `discarded`, or `deferred` first (D-29), because a locked artifact with unjudged alternatives still in the tray is a decision the machine made by silence. Locking then deletes the tray (D-36).

### 6.3a Locks, sign-off, and backtracking

This is the rhythm the Author described, and it is what stops the loop from running endlessly: **work runs to a signature, not until it gives up.**

```
phase N:  crew works (divergence → verdicts → selection → Ideas verified → Author picks Ideas)
              (phase ①: structure → research → sample → screen → engine select → deep review → refine, §16.1)
             ↓  phase Goal passes
          status: pending · pending_kind: phase_signoff      ← the loop STOPS here
             ↓
          Author reads, edits by hand if they like, selects the Ideas they want
             ↓  LOCK
          phase N+1 opens; phase N's artifact is read-only for every agent
             ↓
          ... later, mid phase 3 ...
             ↓
          Author backtracks to phase 2 → every chapter is scrapped (D-25), phase 2 is unlocked and fresh
```

**L-29** When a phase's Goal passes, the loop MUST set `status: pending` with `pending_kind: phase_signoff` and stop dispatching that Story. A phase that keeps improving its artifact past the passing bar is a defect: the artifact is good enough by the Author's own criteria, and only the Author can say what "better" means here (P6).
**L-30** A locked artifact MUST be read-only for every agent, enforced by the **server** at the call (T-1a), not by a prompt and not by hiding the tool. A Writer that tries to `editChapter` against a locked plotline is refused with the lock named.
**D-37** **The Author edits prose in the UI, and only from a paused Story.** The chapter editor is a real editor — the Author types into the book. It is enabled **only while `status: stopped`**, and the UI action that opens it is one button: **Pause & edit**, which stops dispatch, kills in-flight runs (P8), and then unlocks the prose. An edit attempted against a running Story is refused by the server naming D-37, exactly like any other permission failure (T-1a). The reason is not politeness toward the crew: a Writer's `expects` compare-and-swap (T-4) makes a lost race _detectable_, not _resolved_ — the Author would keep losing writes to a machine that retries instantly, and a paragraph half-replaced by each side is the corruption that pausing exists to prevent. Pausing is cheap here because the loop is built to be interrupted and resumed at any point with nothing in memory (L-0, L-3, P8).
**D-37a** **Resume is the hand-back, and it is a commit like any other.** Resuming re-arms the Story to `ready` (subject to the ordinary `pending` checks, L-1) and the Author's edit is processed as a committed change: the Digest is re-derived from it (D-12a), dependents are marked `needs_recheck` by the normal propagation rules (L-11a), and the edited span is marked **Author-written**, so from that point agents may only propose against it (P9, D-20) rather than overwrite it. The loop does not treat the edit as a defect and does not try to restore what it replaced; the next review simply judges the book as it now reads. A directive (D-19) is the Author's way of saying _why_, so the crew propagates the change instead of merely re-judging it.
**D-37b** **Pausing is not a mode the Story enters permanently.** Nothing about a paused Story is special on resume: there is no "author-edited" flag on the Story, no reduced trust in later runs, and no re-approval gate. The crew picks the book up where it stands, which is the same thing that happens after an edit made while the Story was already stopped for another reason (P8, U-12).
**L-31** **Backtrack is Author-only** and is the single way a lock opens (D-25). Backtracking to phase N MUST: unlock phase N, **delete every artifact of every later phase** — chapters and their revisions when backing up to `plotlines`; plotlines, chapters, digests, and per-chapter verdicts when backing up to `summary` — and reset `phase` to N with no memory of the scrapped pass. The `verdicts`, `findings`, and `selection_notes` of the scrapped work survive as provenance (P11); the **prose, the structure, and the Ideas do not** — a phase that was locked already deleted its tray (D-36), and a phase that was never locked loses it with the rest.
**L-32** Backtracking is not undo and MUST NOT be offered as "revert to an earlier draft". It is a phase decision with a confirmation naming exactly what will be deleted, because the alternative — carrying chapters forward that were written against a plotline the Author has since rejected — is the context rot of P2 at book scale.
**L-33** A phase MUST NOT be entered unless every earlier phase is locked. There is no "drafting chapters while the plotlines are still moving": the phase boundary is a hard precondition checked by the sweep, not a convention.

### 6.4 Chapter sequence

**L-8** After the plotlines lock, **all chapters are worked on together by default** — the whole chapter set is in flight at once, not one at a time. Sequential expansion is an option the Author may choose for a short Story, not the default.
**L-8a** Because chapters run together, **consistency is a cross-chapter problem and is handled automatically**: a defect found in chapter 12 that touches something chapter 1 asserts marks chapter 1 `needs_recheck` in the same commit, and the sweep dispatches a Writer to fix whichever side the plotline says is wrong. A Story whose chapters are only ever checked against its own past is a Story that discovers its contradictions at the end (C-4, C-6).
**L-9** The Story MUST carry a declared dependency set (which chapters depend on which) — not to _sequence_ the work, which is what it was for before, but to say **which chapters a change must propagate to**. A chapter with no declared dependency is a chapter nobody will notice has moved.
**L-10** A chapter reaches `done` when its Goal passes **and** the Author accepts, or when the Author marks it done outright ("good enough"). The Author's word outranks the criteria in both directions.
**L-10a** Divergence and selection run concurrently for every chapter, not one chapter at a time (C-B). The unit of concurrency is the chapter; the unit of correctness is the whole chapter set.

### 6.5 Invalidation

**L-11** Within a phase, any commit that changes a Story-level artifact (a world rule, chapter order, an unlocked Summary) MUST mark every dependent plotline and chapter `needs_recheck`, and MUST reopen any chapter already `done` that the change touches. **A change to a _locked_ artifact is not a commit — it is a backtrack** (L-31): the Author unlocks, the later phases are scrapped, and nothing is "rechecked" because there is nothing left to recheck.
**L-11a** Propagation runs **both directions in time**: a contradiction between chapter 1 and chapter 12 marks _both_ `needs_recheck`, and the loop fixes the side the plotline says is wrong. "Fix the later one and hope the earlier one still reads" is not a rule, it is a hope (L-8a).
**L-12** A `needs_recheck` chapter is re-entered into Loop B automatically. Phase ① carries no `needs_recheck` marker: an Author Seed edit or a backtrack re-arms the pipeline from gate 1 (§16.1, D-25a/D-25), because the Summary's samples are rounds, not re-checkable chapters. Without this rule, already-approved chapters rot silently — the exact failure P2 exists to prevent, relocated.

### 6.6 What ends work

There is **no round limit, no token limit, and no time limit** on a loop. A Story may be worked on for days. What ends work, in order of how normal it is:

1. **A phase sign-off** (`pending_kind: phase_signoff`) — the loop reached the bar the Author set and stopped. This is the designed stop, and it happens three times per Story (L-29).
2. **A decision from the Author** (`pending_kind: idea_selection` or `finding_approval`) — Readers have put alternatives on the table, or the Editor has confirmed findings the Story's switch does not auto-approve, and the phase cannot advance until the Author picks or approves (D-29, D-34). Note the qualifier: this wait only begins when **nothing else is dispatchable** (L-36). Until then the pile just grows on the Author's desk while the crew keeps working, which is the intended shape — the loop runs continuously and the Author drains it at their own pace.
   2a. **Drained** (`pending_kind: drained`) — the loop has run out of work it is authorised to do: no task, no unconfirmed finding, and a fresh round of Readers produced nothing at or above the dial threshold (L-37). This is the normal end of a phase's work and the entry point to completion. The Author either picks up the leftovers or marks the Story done.
3. **Author stop** (per Story, or global). Immediate: no new dispatch, in-flight runs killed. A stopped Story never auto-resumes. (P8)
4. **Author marks a chapter or Story done**, overriding a failing Goal.
5. **Completion** — the Story is **drained**, every hard criterion passes, and the Author accepts — which discards whatever findings and Ideas are still open (L-25, L-25b) → `completed` (§6.9). Note that drained (2a) is the loop _stopping_; this is the Author _closing_ it. The loop never completes a Story by itself.
6. **The loop detects it is going in circles** → `pending` with `pending_kind: stalled` (§6.8). Work pauses for the Author; it does not fail.

**L-36** **An unapproved finding does not halt a Story by itself** (D-34). A `confirmed` finding sitting in the queue is a pending _decision_, not a blocked _Story_: the Story stays `ready`, the sweep keeps dispatching everything that does not depend on that decision, and the loop keeps raising, verifying, and queueing more underneath the Author's pile (L-25b).
**L-37** **The loop runs until it is drained, and then it stops.** A Story is **drained** when, on a single full pass over the Story, all of the following hold:

1. no `approved` or `open` task remains for a Writer;
2. no finding remains `unconfirmed` — every suspicion a Reader or checker raised has been confirmed or disputed (D-33);
3. a fresh round of Readers and checkers produced **nothing at or above the dial threshold** (D-35b) — the crew is no longer finding anything it is authorised to work on;
4. no unit still has `competing` revisions awaiting an Editor (D-23).

At that point the sweep sets **`status: pending`, `pending_kind: drained`** and dispatches nothing further. The Story is not complete and not stuck: the crew has run out of work it is allowed to do. The Author now has exactly two moves, and both are theirs to make — **work the leftovers** (approve a sub-threshold finding, which spawns a task and returns the Story to `ready`), or **mark it completed** (L-25, L-26). Condition 3 is what makes drained a real state rather than a momentary gap between sweeps: one clean pass at threshold, with no cost constraint to make it expensive (E-0).
**L-37a** **Drained is checked before stalled, and it wins.** A drained Story has exactly the `trend: flat` signature that L-21 reads as "going in circles" — levels unchanged, no fingerprint closing — so without this precedence a book that has simply run out of problems would be reported to the Author as **stuck**. It is not stuck; it is finished by the standard the dial set. `pending` therefore means "the Author is next" in every case except `stalled`, which is the only one that means trouble.
**L-34** Items 1 and 2 are the loop **working**; item 6 is the loop **stuck**, and the two MUST be distinguishable on screen at a glance. A Story waiting for a signature the Author has not got round to is not the same colour as a Story that has stopped converging.

**L-13** Condition 6 MUST produce an Author-facing explanation on the Pending screen, not a log line.

### 6.7 Loop detection — the measurement

A fresh reviewer every turn (P2) makes "is it improving?" a hard question, because a new reviewer will happily re-report a finding that was already fixed and it will read as a regression. So progress is measured on **stored, deduplicated facts**, never on a run's self-report.

**L-14** The Dashboard maintains, per Story, one **consistency level per phase boundary** (the table in §5.2):

| level                | measured as                                                  |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `idea→summary`       | C-14, C-15 passing, over the Summary draft                   |
| `summary→plotlines`  | hard criteria C-0…C-6 passing, over the plotline set         |
| `plotlines→chapters` | hard criteria C-7…C-9 passing, over the expanded chapter set |

A level for a phase the Story has not reached is `n/a`, not `0/n` — a Story in Phase 1 is not failing at chapters.

**L-15** `trend` is computed from the level history, which is retained per round:

- `improving` — any level rose, or an open finding fingerprint closed;
- `flat` — **every** level unchanged for `flat_rounds` consecutive rounds **and** no finding fingerprint closed;
- `regressing` — any level fell, or a previously-closed fingerprint reopened.

**L-15a** Only **confirmed** findings count in `trend` (D-33). An amnesiac fresh reviewer that keeps raising the same _unconfirmed_ suspicion is not evidence that the Story is stuck, and confirming it is the Editor's job — not a reason to page the Author.

Only levels the Story has actually reached are consulted: a Story in Phase 1 cannot be judged on a chapter level that does not exist yet.

**L-16a** A re-reported finding is **not** a regression: it increments `seen_count` on the existing fingerprint (D-16). Only a fingerprint that had been _closed_ and came back counts as going backwards. Without this rule, an amnesiac reviewer makes every long-running Story look like it is failing.

### 6.8 Going in circles → `pending`

**L-21a** `pending` has two causes and they are different machines: an **awaiting-the-Author wait** — sign-off, Idea pick, finding approval, or **drained** (L-29, D-29, D-34, L-36, L-37) — which is planned and expected, and a **stall** (L-21), which is not. Only the stall consults `trend`; the wait consults nothing but the queue, and it ends the moment the Author acts.
**L-21** Only after L-37 has been ruled out: when `trend` is `flat` for `flat_rounds` rounds, or `regressing` beyond `regress_tolerance` — counting only **confirmed** findings (L-15a) — the Story moves to **`status: pending`** and the sweep stops dispatching it (D-2a). The Author's tree shows it as needing review, with the reason.
**L-22** `status_reason` MUST name the specific stuck thing (for a sign-off wait, the artifact awaiting the lock and the empty Ideas tray count): which level, which criteria will not move, which finding fingerprints have been open the longest **and are confirmed rather than disputed**, which chapters are involved, and what the crews tried. "Consistency low" is not a reason; "C-4 has failed on chapters 6–9 for 11 rounds; the crews keep moving the mentor scene and the reader keeps objecting to it" is.
**L-23** The stalled screen MUST offer the Author the four exits, as buttons: **edit the unlocked contract, or backtrack to the phase that owns it** (the contract was wrong), **edit a template's instructions or `selection_rules`** (the crew's taste or its brief was wrong), **issue a directive** (the crew needed a decision), **lower the bar** (this criterion does not matter to me). Every one of those re-arms the Story to `ready`.
**L-24** Neither kind of `pending` is an error state, and neither MUST be rendered as one. A Story waiting for a signature is the product working; a Story that stopped converging is the product being honest. A Story that goes pending after four days of good work is the system working correctly: it found something only the Author can settle.

### 6.9 Completion

**L-25** The loop **proposes** `completed` when the Story is **drained** (L-37) and, simultaneously: every **hard** criterion passes at every level; the ledger has no `conflict` or `open`-past-deadline entries; and the soft-pass count is at or above `threshold_soft`. Drained is the trigger — the loop stops looking for work and tells the Author so — and the criteria are what makes the stop worth accepting rather than just quiet.
**L-25b** **Completion does not require an empty findings list.** The loop runs continuously and will always find something; a bar of "zero open findings" is a bar no book clears, and it would leave the Story working forever on `minor` and `taste` items the Author does not care about. What it needs is the **Author's acceptance** (L-26). Whatever is still open at that moment — findings, `proposed` Ideas — is **discarded by the act of completing**, and the count is recorded and shown, so the Author can see what they waved away rather than pretend it was never raised.
**L-25a** `completed` **is Phase 3's lock**: the Author's confirmation locks the chapters exactly as the Summary and plotline locks work, and the sweep stops dispatching. There is no fourth phase.
**L-26** Completion is then **confirmed by the Author** — the loop proposes it, the Author accepts it, and the acceptance is what closes the remaining findings and Ideas (L-25b). This is the last place the machine gets to overrule a human, and the first place a human is allowed to overrule a very long to-do list.
**L-27** `completed` is not permanent. Any Author edit (made from a pause and applied on resume — D-37), any `needs_recheck`, or any directive returns the Story to `ready`, and the sweep picks it up again. A finished book the Author keeps writing by hand stays policed forever — which is the point of a loop that never runs out of time.
**L-28** "All the agents agreed" means the _hard_ criteria pass under independent fresh review, not that no reviewer has an opinion. A Reader with a `taste`-cost finding open does not block completion (D-16, G-8).
**D-39** **A completed Story is a dead end on purpose.** Nothing is exported from it and nothing is inherited by the next Story: no taste record, no "what this Author always rejects", no carry-over of the discarded Ideas the lock already deleted (D-36). The records — verdicts, findings, `selection_note`s, the run history — survive for audit and for the Author to read, and they are read by nobody automatically. A sequel is **not** a continuation of state: it is a new Story, and the feature it needs (deferred, §13) is giving its phase ① crews **read access to the prequel's artifacts** so the Writer, Editor and Readers can form the new book against the old one. That is the whole design reserved for later, and it is deliberately not built now: it is the one case that breaks the rule that a run is scoped to exactly one Story (T-3), so it gets an explicit exception when it is designed rather than a half-built second scoping path now. The Seed stays the only cross-book thing the product offers in v1 — and copying it by hand is already how siblings work (D-25a).

Every tool is registered everywhere (T-6a), but the **server** enforces the row: a Reader's `editChapter`, an Editor's prose write, and any agent's write to a locked artifact are refused at the call, naming the rule and the artifact (T-1). Permission is enforced at commit time, not by hiding the tool — the refusal is a teaching message inside the run, and the run changes tactic.
**T-1c** **The Researcher's column** (§16.3): it reads the Seed, the Summary and every other story artifact, and it **writes `knowledge`** and may file a **`tasks`** row (D-11); it also holds the `proposals` write every kind holds (P9's propose-only direction) and `recommend` on `sub_agent_runs`, but it **reads** `verdicts`, `findings` and `ideas` because it does not judge prose. A Researcher write to a story artifact is refused naming P10, exactly like a Checker's or a Reader's. The three phase-① collections follow the same rule: `seed_briefs` is the **Editor's** write and read for every other kind; `knowledge` is the Researcher's; `sample_reviews` is **written by the engine** from the screening answer and read by every kind (§16.2, §16.5).

**T-0** Role kind on the template (`roles[].kind`) names the **employee** the role assigns, and selects the brief and the permission row — all kinds get the full tool catalogue (T-6a). **How many roles of each employee kind a Story has is the Author's choice** (D-38a): the loop never hard-codes one Writer or one Reader, and `max_concurrent` / `attempts` / `agents` are template settings, not engine constants. Every built-in employee is Rank A and may own a Work Item; a Rank B employee may be added only as a delegation target (V-2). The only hard requirements are structural: at least one Editor available once `attempts > 1` **or once any Reader or checker is assigned** — an Editor is not optional when there is more than one draft to choose between, and it is not optional when somebody else is filing findings the loop is about to act on (D-33) — and a unit with competing revisions waits for a selection (D-23).
**T-1** The tool layer enforces this table. A tool call that violates it is refused with a message naming the rule and the artifact, and the refusal is visible to the agent so it can change tactic rather than retry.
**T-2** Author-written spans are tracked at section granularity (D-7), so P9 is enforceable without freezing the whole chapter.

## 8. Tools

Tools are the only hands the crew has (P1, P3). **Every session is built with the whole catalogue** — §8.1 through §8.5 and §8.7's knowledge group, web tools included — and the server refuses the calls an employee kind may not make. What separates a Writer from a Reader from an Editor is therefore **its instructions and its permission row, not its tool list** (D-17a, T-1a). A crew that can see `editChapter` and is told not to use it behaves better than one that cannot see it and invents a workaround.

One execution mode is a **named exception to uniform registration**, and it is a subtraction at dispatch, never a permission: a run may be dispatched with `toolMode: 'none'` — a single assistant turn with an empty offered-tool list and a `response_format` contract. The catalogue stays uniform (T-6a); only that run's offered list is empty. Phase ①'s sampling and screening runs are the only users (§16.4).

Two obligations come with that, and they are build obligations, not prompt policy: the catalogue must **reach everything a brief can ask for** (T-12), and it must be **readable from inside the run** (T-6c). A Writer, a Reader, and an Editor all start from the same place — the whole surface, listed, describable, and complete enough that the job in front of them is always something they can actually do.

**T-3** A run is spawned with `storyId` bound into its tool context. No story tool accepts a `storyId` argument — the runtime supplies it — so an agent physically cannot read or write another Story's records. A run exists for exactly one Story for exactly one Work Item.

### 8.1 The Writer surface

Every session sees these tools (T-6a); the **Writer is the only role the server lets use them** on a story artifact. It writes the Summary, the plotlines, the chapters, and the prose — everything that becomes part of the book.

**T-3a** The Writer MUST be able to run to completion without the Author: it keeps the task queue as its work list and keeps writing until the queue is empty or it must escalate (S-4). "Regardless of number of turns" is a property of the queue, not of a single run.

**T-2b** Delegation stays inside one run and one Story: a delegated sub-context is not a Work Item, does not appear in the crew panel as a dispatched run, and cannot write anything the parent could not.

**T-4** Every mutating tool takes `expects: [{id, rev}]` compare-and-swap. A rejected write returns the current record so the agent re-reads and re-applies instead of forcing. Concurrent crews are expected, not exceptional.
**T-5** Every mutating tool takes `why` and records it (P10).
**T-6** Tools MUST be registered on the harness at session build (host-side `registerTool`/`addTool`), declared `core` so a fresh thread sees them on turn one with no discovery round trip — a fresh thread every turn (P2) cannot afford a discovery turn.
**T-6a** Registration is **uniform**: every session, whatever its `kind`, gets §8.1 + §8.2 + §8.3 + §8.4 + §8.5 + §8.7. `kind` selects the **brief and the permission row**, not the tool list (D-17a). One execution mode subtracts the list at dispatch — `toolMode: 'none'`, phase ①'s `draft_groups`/`review_groups` passes (§17.2, S-8) and, on a legacy book, its samples and screens (§16.4) — and it is a mode, not a permission. This is deliberate: a fresh context every turn (P2) that cannot see a tool does not abstain, it improvises — and an improvised workaround is invisible to the Author, while a refused call is a record.
**T-6c** **The catalogue is self-describing inside the run.** Every session carries `listTools` and `describeTool`. Presence is free — the names are already visible at turn one (T-6) — so discovery is never "does this tool exist"; it is "what exactly does it take", answerable in one call instead of by guessing and being refused (T-11). This is not the discovery round trip T-6 forbids: presence costs nothing, only detail is on demand.
**T-12** **The catalogue must cover the brief.** No Work Item is dispatched whose job has no reachable tool: before a template may ask for an action, §8.1–§8.5 and §8.7 must contain a call that performs it. When a run finds its brief asking for something the surface cannot do, it calls `reportGap` — naming the job, what it needed, and what it did instead — and the Story is not penalised in loop detection for the shortfall. A gap is a defect in the **build**, not in the crew. The alternative is the failure T-6a already describes: the agent improvises, the workaround looks like a choice, and the Author never learns a tool was missing.
**T-13** **Capabilities are registered tools, not prompt prose.** Everything §8 names is a host-side registered tool with a schema the server validates (T-6, T-11), present in every session (T-6a). Nothing in the story surface may be "the agent should know how" or "the instructions tell it to": a rule with no tool behind it is a rule a fresh context will eventually violate, and P2 guarantees a fresh context every turn. The Author's assistant (§8.6) and the `toolMode: 'none'` execution mode (§16.4) are the only exceptions to uniform registration, and both are subtraction.
**T-6b** **Web tools are available to every role**, and what governs their use is the template's instructions (D-30). A Reader is told to research in order to form and check ideas; a Writer may look up whether a revolver in 1880 could actually fire six times; a checker may confirm a period fact before it files the finding. Cost is no object (E-0), so the constraint is not _who may browse_ but **what counts as evidence**: a claim that reaches a locked artifact needs an Editor's verdict (C-15), whoever fetched it.

**T-9** A Reader MUST NOT write a story artifact — it _can_ call `editChapter`, and the server will refuse it (T-1a). Its accepted output is `proposeIdea`, `raiseFinding`, and `submitVerdict` (D-26, P10). A Reader that rewrites chapter 4 has destroyed the independence the phase model buys it, and the refusal is what stops it from doing so quietly.
**D-30** A Reader role's instructions name **what it reads for and what it may propose**: rate the chapter as the Author reads it, flag anything that diverges from the plotline, anything that references something that does not hold up, anything that spends a mystery before it is earned — and propose freely, including ideas that are wrong. The **Editor** is what keeps those proposals inside the Author's guidelines (D-31), not a limit on what a Reader is allowed to think about.
**T-11** **The tool validates the request body, and the agent sees the error.** `cost` is a required enum on `raiseFinding`, so a Reader cannot file an untriaged finding; `evidence` is required on a failing criterion (D-15); `selection_note` is required on a selection (T-2a). A malformed call is refused naming the field and the expected shape, and the run corrects it in place — schema enforcement belongs in the tool, not in the prompt, because a prompt is something a fresh context can be tired of.
**T-10** Every `sources[]` entry records `url_or_title`, the specific `claim` it supports, and `accessed_at`. An Idea citing a page for a claim that page does not make is `refuted` by the Editor, not "mostly right" (C-15).
**T-7** Tool results are bounded and structured; a tool that hands a whole book to a reviewer breaks the reading economy (§11) and is a defect, not a convenience.
**T-8** Every tool result names the record revision it was read at, so a follow-up call in the same run can send a valid `expects` without re-reading.

The Author's assistant (invoked by the Author, on a Summary or a Directive) gets the Author's write rights for the duration of that one run, and nothing else. It does not get the Reader's web tools, and it never writes a locked artifact.

## 9. Run lifecycle

```
sweep → Story is `ready`? → WorkItem claimed (lease)
      → spawn: open one fresh thread on the employee kind the role names;
               storyId bound, brief assembled
      → run:  the employee acts as an agent through tools, no chat
              (an Editor may delegate to fresh sub-contexts inside this run, D-17e;
               a `toolMode: 'none'` run makes one provider call instead — §16.5)
      → report: submitVerdict / raiseFinding / createTask / requestAuthor / proposeIdea
                selectRevision / requestMerge / rejectAll / verifyIdea
                (a `toolMode: 'none'` run's report is its validated structured
                 answer, which the engine persists — §16.5)
      → commit: server applies, stamps, deletes non-selected revisions (D-22),
                invalidates dependents, rebuilds digest
      → teardown: thread destroyed, run row closed, the employee returns to idle
      → findings: confirmed by an Editor (D-33) → rewritten for the Author (D-34)
                → approved by the Author, or by the switch if the cost is covered (D-35)
                → task → dispatched to a Writer on a later sweep
      → recompute the phase's level and trend
            → phase Goal passed?  → `pending` · `phase_signoff`: STOP, wait for the lock (L-29)
            → Ideas or confirmed findings awaiting the Author, and nothing else to dispatch?
              → `pending` · `idea_selection` / `finding_approval` (D-29, D-34, L-36)
            → drained? (no task, no unconfirmed finding, one clean round at
              or above the dial threshold) → `pending` · `drained` (L-37)
            → else trend flat or regressing? → `pending` · `stalled` (§6.8, L-37a)
      → next sweep
```

**S-1** One Work Item = one Run = one fresh thread. A thread is never handed a second job, and no thread is ever reused. The employee is the standing identity; the thread is the agent execution it causes; the phase's phrase session outlives both.
**S-2** The brief is assembled by the server from the store: `role prompt + phase + Goal criteria for that phase + the Seed + Digest + the artifact under review + the specific job + what changed since the last pass + the open tasks that job must clear + the selected Ideas this run must absorb (D-29)`. A Reader's brief adds the research question and the sources already cited, so two Readers do not browse to the same page. It MUST be self-contained; a thread has no memory of any prior thread. A `toolMode: 'none'` run is the named exception: it is given the **compiled prompt** instead of the assembled brief — the Seed Brief, the `critical`/`high` Knowledge, and (from round 2) the base revision and the open fix tasks (§16.5) — because its whole job is one structured answer, not an action on the book.
**S-3** Teardown is unconditional — including on failure, timeout, and Author stop. No orphan threads. The run row records why it ended.
**S-4** A run that ends without calling `submitVerdict`, `raiseFinding`, `createTask`, `requestAuthor`, `proposeIdea`, `verifyIdea`, `selectRevision`, or `requestMerge` is a **no-op turn**: it MUST count toward loop detection (§6.7 / L-15). A Writer run that ends without writing anything and without escalating is a bug in its brief, not a cost.
**S-4a** There is no timeout on a run and no retry cap. A run that takes four hours is fine. A run that hangs is detected by **lease expiry** (L-2), not by a clock: the lease lapses, the Work Item returns to the queue, and the run is torn down.
**S-5** Concurrency: `max_concurrent` per template, per Story, and a global cap. The sweep fills capacity across all Stories, so the crew stays busy whenever any Story has work (the Author's "constantly busy" requirement).
**S-6** A run's wall-clock and token usage are recorded and surfaced per Story, per chapter, per template — **informationally only**. Nothing may read them as a limit (E-0). They answer "why is this taking an hour?", not "can we afford this?"
**S-7** A delegated sub-context (D-17e) is not a Work Item and gets no row of its own in the crew panel; it is shown nested under the run that spawned it, because the Author's unit of attention and of control is the dispatched run.
**S-8** The grouped Summary's two no-tool job kinds (§17). `draft_groups` (Writer) answers `GroupDraft` — one `{group_ref, content}` per group in its batch — and `review_groups` (Reader) answers `GroupReview`, every issue naming the groups it crosses. Both run with `toolMode: 'none'` (§16.4), so their report is the validated structured answer and the engine does the write. What the engine does with a `draft_groups` answer is the redesign's whole swap: **install, archive, recompose, mirror — in the commit that closes the run** (§4.19, R2/R5/R6). There is no selection step, no `selectRevision`, no `requestMerge` and no "reject all and re-brief" for a grouped Summary (S-4's report verbs simply never appear on such a run), and an answer that fails the schema is refused for the WHOLE batch (`phrase/revisions.ts:parseGroupDraftAnswer`): half an Overview is not an Overview.
**S-9** The grouped Summary's bounds, and why they are not caps in E-0's sense (R4, R5). A Writer batch is `max(1, stories.summary_sampling)` groups; a group whose text is current gets **two** rewrite attempts before the loop stops paying for it (`GROUP_WRITE_ATTEMPT_CAP`, counted over closed HOLDING runs against that group's `latest` revision — never over a live run, never over a reading seat); a group an unresolvable defect names stays open in the review rail with its attempts visible, which is the escalation. None of these bounds a phase transition or halts a loop: they stop ONE group from being rewritten forever while the rest of the Summary keeps working, and the Author's own knobs (`summary_sampling`, `summary_readers`, the dial) still set how much is asked for.

### 10.2 Requirements

**U-1 Stories panel.** Every Story shows its **phase** (① Summary · ② Plotlines · ③ Chapters, with a lock icon on each phase it has signed), its **`status` badge** (`ready` / `pending` / `completed` / `stopped`), its consistency levels for the phases it has reached (`idea→summary 2/2 · summary→plotlines 6/6 · plotlines→chapters n/a`), its `trend` arrow, and `status_reason` when pending. **Stories waiting on the Author sort to the top** — they are the ones only the Author can move.
**U-1b** A `pending` Story is **two different colours**: awaiting the Author — signoff, idea pick, finding approval, or **drained** — is calm (the loop finished something, ran out of authorised work, or is waiting to be told), stalled is urgent (the loop hit a wall). L-34, L-36, L-37a. Awaiting-signoff is the state the whole product aims at, and it is the one the Author should be able to clear in one click.
**U-1c** **Drained reads as a finished thing, not an idle one**: the row says what the crew stopped finding ("no critical or major left · 12 minor and 4 taste awaiting you") and offers the two moves L-37 allows — **pick up the leftovers** or **complete**. The word "drained" should not appear on screen; it is the internal name for "we are done with what we were told to do."
**U-1a** The **stalled** screen is the Author's review surface, not a log: it shows the stuck criteria, the oldest open findings, the chapters involved, what the crews tried across rounds, and the four exits from L-23 as buttons.
**U-2** A Story with nothing blocked still shows what the crew is doing to it right now.
**U-3** Per-Story Stop / Resume, and one global Stop. Global Stop is the biggest button on the screen.
**U-4 The Story view.** The **Seed** is a pinned, read-only card at the top of every Story for every viewer — it never scrolls away, because it is the thing the book is measured against (C-14). The Summary pane is editable by the Author until the lock; on a grouped Story the Author's hand goes on a **group**, one at a time (U-18) — phase ①'s prose is never typed into the pane, because the engine installs what it accepts (§4.19 on a grouped book, §16.6 on a legacy one) — and once locked it is read-only with an "Ask assistant" affordance and a **Backtrack** button that says exactly what it will delete (L-32). Plotlines and chapters are shown side by side — the contract next to the prose — because the whole product is the distance between them.
**U-5** Chapter prose shows inline accept/reject per proposal, with the `why` and the run that made it, and undo. A diff, not a replaced paragraph.
**U-17 The prose is editable, and the pause is the price.** The chapter pane is a text editor, greyed out while the Story runs, with one button that makes it live: **Pause & edit** (D-37). It states what pausing costs — runs killed, dispatch halted — and **Resume** states what the edit will trigger: digest re-derived, N dependents marked for recheck (D-37a). While paused, the pane says so at the top ("paused by you · 3 runs stopped"), because a Story that looks idle and is actually being edited by hand is a different thing from one the crew has drained (U-1c).
**U-6** Goal panel: the criteria list with live pass/fail per criterion, the failing evidence, the `fix_hint`, and the Author's controls (toggle hard, delete, add, threshold, per-chapter bar override).
**U-7 Crew panel.** Live threads: the employee kind (Writer / Editor / Reader / Checker / Researcher), template, what the thread is doing, which task it holds, elapsed, and a kill button per thread. A row is one thread, not the employee itself; the employee is idle when it has no row.
**U-8 Template panel.** The catalogue. Each card: roles naming their employee `kind` (Writer / Editor / Reader / Checker / Researcher) and whether a Reader role has `web`, phase it serves, done-definition, `attempts`, escalation rule. Actions: **New**, **Duplicate**, **Edit roles and prompts**, **Set agents per role** (D-38), **Set attempts**, **Assign to phase**, **Assign as a Writer of this Story** — repeatable, since the count is the Author's call (D-17b, D-38a). Built-ins duplicate-only (D-17). The employee roster above the catalogue is global; it lists every employee once, alphabetically (Checker · Editor · Reader · Researcher · Writer), with Writer given no priority — every Story's threads run as agents under it.
**U-8a** The Writer employee (and any template role of that kind) is visually distinct from the checkers — it is the only one that produces prose, and the Author should never confuse the crew that writes with the crew that judges.
**U-8b** An Editor template's card shows its **`selection_rules` in the Author's own words**, editable in place, because that text _is_ the Author's automated taste (P12). Editing it and watching the next `selection_note` change is the core tuning loop of the product.
**U-8c** Each template card shows `attempts` — how many competing drafts it spawns — since that is the dial that turns divergence on and off.
**U-8d** Each role row shows `agents: N` and its `aggregate` mode, since "this Editor is three agents of that employee kind, arguing and returning one answer" (D-38) is invisible unless the card says so. When a run's agents disagreed, the crew panel marks that run: the dissent belongs in the `selection_note`, and the Author needs to know which authoritative answers were unanimous.

**U-9 Directive bar.** A single line of Author intent addressed to the Story's **Editor**, which becomes a `directives` record and propagates (D-19). Not a chat box: it does not accept conversation, it accepts decisions. It sits at the bottom of the content region; on a phone it is the harness's minimal composer — one field and a send control, docked to the viewport.
**U-10 The Author's queue.** Confirmed findings awaiting approval, Ideas awaiting a pick, and escalations, aggregated across all Stories, are the home screen. "3 stories need you." This is the loop-engineering aimed at the human (D-34).
**U-10a** Each finding in the queue reads as the **Editor wrote it**, not as the reviewer wrote it: the claim, the evidence line, the cost, and two buttons — approve, discard (D-34). The Story view shows its **auto-approve dial** beside Stop, with the costs it currently skips (D-35), because the Author should never be surprised later by work the machine started on their behalf.
**U-10b** Approving is progressive and never batched away silently: the Author may approve one finding, approve a whole chapter's worth, or turn on auto-approve for a cost and stop touching that class of problem. The queue drains at whatever pace the Author sets, and the loop keeps finding more underneath them (L-25b).
**U-11 Nothing on screen is agent activity for its own sake.** No "loop running ✓" spinner. Progress reads as story: chapters drafted, hole in act 2, setups unpaid, arcs unresolved.
**U-12** The Dashboard is usable with zero harnesses running: the Author can write the Seed, a Summary, plotlines, or chapter prose by hand at any time, and the crew simply picks it up on the next sweep. With harnesses running, chapter prose still needs the pause first (U-17, D-37) — the rule is about racing, not about permission.
**U-12a** The **phase strip** — ① Summary · ② Plotlines · ③ Chapters, each with a lock icon when signed — is the first thing on the Story view and the second thing on each Stories-panel row. The Author should be able to tell where any Story is in the pipeline from across the room.
**U-13 Revision comparison.** When a unit has competing revisions, the Story view shows them **side by side** with the checkers' verdicts and the Editor's `selection_note`. The Author can select, merge, or restore any of them before the Editor commits (D-24), and can watch the losers disappear on selection (D-22). **This requirement is about plotlines, chapters, and a legacy Summary.** A grouped Summary has no competing set to lay side by side: its comparison surface is per group — the current text beside the COUNT of its superseded revisions (§4.19) — and a group's history is read, never deleted (R2). The Select/Merge controls therefore do not appear on a grouped Summary, and the routes refuse those verbs there rather than pretending (R5).
**U-15 The Ideas tray.** Every `proposed` Idea for the current phase, in one place, **above a collapsed list of what has already been decided** — selected, discarded, deferred, each with its `selection_note` (D-32). The tray is **phase-scoped and empties at the lock** (D-36); it is a workspace, not an archive. The decided list is not decoration: it is the only reason the next fresh Reader does not propose the same thing again, and the Author should see the crew re-raising a settled point as the repeat badge it is. who filed it, what it claims, the sources it cites, and the Editor's verdict with evidence (`verified` · `refuted` · `unverifiable`). The Author **selects** or **discards** each one; a selected Idea becomes the next Writer's brief (D-29). Unverified Ideas are visibly dimmed and cannot be selected without an explicit "I know" (D-27). The tray shows a count, and the phase cannot be locked while it is non-empty (L-7a).
**U-16 Lock and backtrack are the two biggest buttons on the Story view.** **Lock phase N** appears only when that phase's Goal passes and the Ideas tray is empty; it states in one line what becomes read-only. **Backtrack to phase N** states in one line what gets deleted, and asks once (L-31, L-32). There is no other way to change a locked artifact, and the UI should say so on every locked pane.
**U-14 Completion is a proposal.** A `completed` Story shows what made it complete — the passing levels, the ledger state — **and exactly what accepting will discard**: "47 findings and 12 Ideas will be closed without action, the oldest from chapter 2" (L-25b). The Author accepts with their eyes open, and the count is kept as a record afterwards. A green tick the machine gave itself is not information; neither is a to-do list quietly emptied.

**U-18 The Summary workspace is the five groups, not one text (2026-11-24, R2/R4/R6).** The Summary pane is the composed **Overview** plus one pane per group. Each group pane states, from the `GET …/phrases` read alone: the group's own **revision number** (the number the rail stopped printing — a group's number belongs in its own pane, never in the rail), its text, what it still owes (the open findings that name it and have no revision of ITS resolving them, R3), and its superseded count. The pane's one write is **Revise this group**: `POST …/phrases/groups/:groupId/revise` with the new text, which installs a revision and archives the one it replaces exactly as a Writer install does (R2). While a Writer pass holds the group the control is **disabled**, and the pane says why in the same sentence the route's 409 uses — the button and the refusal read the same `writer_busy` fact, so neither can be stale about the other (R4/F8). No Select, no Merge, no whole-Summary "ask for another round", and no sampling dial on this surface: those belong to a legacy book's pane, and their routes answer 409 here naming the verb that works. The pane shows the latest text and the **count** of superseded revisions — the wire carries `chars`, not the archived prose, and a row the reader cannot open is decoration (U3).
**U-19 The sign-off gate tells the truth about a grouped Summary (R1/R3/R4).** The desk MUST NOT present a Summary as ready while the phrase read says otherwise: while the phrase read has not landed (in flight or failed — an absent read is not an empty valid Phrase), while any group is unwritten, while a blocking-cost finding is open on any group, while a task is open in the queue (phrased as the Story's task, because the read's task rows carry no group attribution), or while a live Writer is rewriting a group. Each blocker is phrased as the wait it is, with the group's title, and the list is derived from the same rows the engine acts on (`story-contract.ts:groupSignOffBlockers`). What the desk may NOT do is invent a gate the server ignores: the HTTP lock stays exactly §6.3a/§10.2/U-16's gate (the Ideas tray, then the phase Goal), because a blocker the UI claims and the route denies is a button that lies. The one group truth the desk does NOT print as a sentence is the owed reading: the Reader seats and deep passes the CURRENT vector still owes are the engine's hold, not a UI blocker — the sweep withholds the `phase_signoff` offer over them (`status.ts:decideStory` → `phrase/exclusion.ts:groupPipelineOwed`, D-62), so the Story simply stays `ready` and the drain line names the passes. A rail blocker for that would be a second, looser copy of a rule the server already enforces.

## 11. Reading economy — cost is out of scope

**E-0** Neither tokens nor wall-clock are design constraints. Unlimited both, by decision. **No budget, cap, timeout, or round limit may gate a phase transition, halt a loop, or be used to argue against a design.** A Story may be worked on for days; that is expected, not a problem to solve.

**E-0a** The only scarcity the design honours is **attention and correctness**: the Author's time, and the risk that a fresh context reads too much and loses the thread. Four things replace every budget as the thing that stops work: the **phase sign-off** (L-29), the **approval wait** (D-34), and **drained** (L-37) — all three designed — and **loop detection** (§6.8), the unplanned one. Note what is _not_ on that list: an unfinished to-do list. The loop keeps finding work until the Author says the book is done (L-25b) — the throttle is the Author's attention, spent through the auto-approve dial (D-35), never a cap on how much may be noticed.
**E-0b** Web reads are free in the same sense (D-27 aside): a Reader MAY fetch twenty pages to support one Idea, and any other role may look something up before it acts on it (T-6b). The constraint on research is **verification, not volume** — an unverified claim is worthless however well-sourced, and a verified one costs nothing extra to have.
**E-0c** **Sources are not archived.** A citation only has to hold for the duration of the Story being written (D-27): a book is produced in days, published or posted, and then the Author starts the next one. Link rot after publication is not this product's problem, and neither is a citation library. `accessed_at` is recorded and that is all.

What still matters is **reading economy**, because a fresh context that re-reads the whole book every turn produces _worse judgments_, not merely slower ones.

**E-1** A run's required reading is `Digest + the artifact under review + the criteria`. Roughly constant regardless of book length.
**E-2** A run that reads more than its brief asked for is a `digest_gap` signal, so the Digest improves rather than the reading silently growing.
**E-3** A run's brief MUST fit the job it is given: one judgment, or one bounded edit set. An oversized brief is what reintroduces context rot _inside_ a single run, which P2 exists to prevent.

**C-B** All chapters are in flight together by default (L-8), which is safe only because every one of them is a separate session (S-1). Since cost is out of scope, expense is never a reason to sequence; the dependency set exists to say **where a fix must propagate** (L-9), not what may run when.
**C-C** There is no per-Work-Item, per-chapter, or per-Story budget. The stops are the **phase sign-off** (L-29, the designed one) and **loop detection** at Story level (§6.8, the unplanned one), and both pause rather than fail.
**E-4** Divergence is cheap and valuable: `attempts` defaults above 1 wherever an Editor template is assigned, because comparing drafts is the mechanism the whole architecture exists to provide (P13).

## 13. Non-goals

- **Not a chat client.** There is no conversation surface for the loop. The composer is a directive bar addressed to the Editor; its answer is the queue changing, never a reply.
- **Not an agent list.** The right panel names the global employees and the crew templates. An agent is an employee only while its thread is executing; it is never a roster entry.
- **No reviewer prose.** A checker never writes a chapter, a plotline, or a line of dialogue. It files a finding and a task.
- **No Editor prose.** An Editor selects and merges structure; it never composes a sentence (L-19).
- **No best-story ambition.** The target is the Author's own story, automated (P12, G-8). A criterion that chases objective quality is a build defect.
- **No surviving rejected drafts.** Non-selected revisions are deleted (P13, D-22).
- **No agent chooses the chapter count.** The Author declares it, and it is read-only for every agent at every phase (D-3b).
- **No token budget, no time budget, no round cap.** Cost is out of scope by decision, not by oversight.
- **No silent spinning.** A loop that stops converging says so and waits (P14, §6.8).
- **No long-lived agent.** An employee becomes an agent only for the thread its Work Item opens; no thread outlives its Run.
- **No agent edits a _locked_ Summary**, and **no agent edits the Seed** — those are the Author's, and the only way in is a backtrack.
- **No Reader writes prose.** A Reader researches, rates, and proposes; it never edits — and it never decides that its own Idea is good enough (D-26, T-9).
- **No unconfirmed suspicion costs a Writer a pass.** A finding from a Reader or checker is a claim until an Editor verifies it (D-33).
- **No completion requires an empty findings list.** The loop always finds something; completion needs no work left _at the dial threshold_, not zero findings, and the Author's acceptance closes the remainder with a count on it (L-25b, L-37).
- **No appeal above the Editor's triage.** It sets the cost, disputes what it doubts, and that is the end of it; the Author edits the template, not the finding (D-34a).
- **No reporting a finished book as stuck.** Drained outranks stalled (L-37a): running out of problems is not going in circles.
- **No discarded Idea is deleted inside the phase that decided it.** A decision is memory, not an alternative (D-28, D-32) — and it is memory for that phase only (D-36).
- **No Idea outlives its phase.** Locking deletes the tray; the artifact is what remains (D-36).
- **No finding reaches the Writer unapproved.** Reader raises, Editor verifies and rewrites, Author approves — unless the Story's switch approved it for them (D-34, D-35).
- **No tool is hidden from a role.** Every session sees the whole catalogue; the server says no (T-6a, T-1a).
- **Nothing is a prompt-only rule.** Every capability §8 names is a registered, schema-validated tool. If the surface cannot do something a brief asks for, that is a build gap to be reported and filled (T-12), not something to exhort a fresh context into (T-13).
- **No phase runs past its sign-off.** A Goal that passes stops the loop (L-29); there is no "keep polishing until the Author notices".
- **No carrying work across a backtrack.** Scrapped phases leave prose behind; only their records survive (D-25).
- **No numeric quality score.** Criteria only.
- **No autonomous sign-off.** The Author locks each of the three phases and marks work done. A phase that passes its Goal waits; it does not proceed.
- **No multi-story run.** A run exists for exactly one Story.
- **Nothing survives a finished book.** `completed` means completed: no taste record, no carry-over of what the Author rejected, no export, no lessons. A book that is done leaves records behind for audit and nothing else (D-25a, D-39).
- **No sequel awareness in the first build.** A sequel is a new Story whose phase ① crews are given **read access to the prequel** — that is the shape of it, and it is deferred, not designed (D-39). It is the one place the single-Story scoping rule (T-3) will need an explicit exception, which is why it stays out of v1 rather than being half-built.
- **No agent-authored Digest.** The Writer writes everything in the book; the Digest is not in the book — it is derived from commits by the engine (D-12a).
- **No Author prose while the crew is running.** The chapter editor is live only from a pause (D-37); the product does not attempt live collaborative editing.
- **No recipes in the first build.** Templates carry it. A recipe is what gets named once several Stories have been built the same way by hand (P15).
- **No engine rewrite needed to change the design.** Not a non-goal so much as a build constraint (P15): a phase model, a role set, or a criterion must be replaceable without rewriting the store.

## 15. Vertical slice — the thing to build first

Prove the loop before building twelve panels. One Story, all three phases, in this order:

0. Author writes a **Seed** — three sentences — and `chapter_count` = 8. Every employee can read it; nobody can write it. **Steps 1 and 1a are the LEGACY walk** (§16): they describe a `summary_mode: 'sampling'` book. A Story created now is grouped, and its step 1 instead runs §17: the same two research jobs, then one tool-less `draft_groups` pass writes all five groups and INSTALLS on acceptance (§17.4), `summary_readers` reading seats review the group vector (§17.5), the four-angle deep review files findings that must name their groups (R3), and the approved `fix` task routes the NEXT batch to exactly the groups it names — with no select, no merge, no truncation, and no round counter anywhere.
1. **Phase ①.** The sweep runs the research pipeline in order (§16.1). An **Editor** job structures the Seed (`structureSeed` → one `accepted` Seed Brief, `seed_hash` matching the Seed, and the `research_questions` list that states — explicitly, T-11 if omitted — whether the book needs research at all: `[]` waives the leg, D-51). A **Researcher** job researches that brief and records two sourced `knowledge` entries — one `critical`, one `high` — with `writeKnowledge`; a pass that finds nothing to record closes itself with `reportNoResearch` (zero writes, and a report the loop counts — D-51). Three tool-less one-turn **Writer** samples then expand the brief (no tools, one `SummarySample` JSON object each) and five tool-less **Reader** passes screen each sample with an `IssueList`. The **engine** selects the least-issue sample, installs its six prose fields on the Summary — never `chapter_count` — appends the round to `sampling_history`, hard-deletes the losing samples and their reviews, and advances `sampling_round`, all in one commit (§16.5, §16.6). The `idea→summary` levels read from the installed Summary.
   1a. The selected revision gets the real review: five tool-using `wi_review_*` **Reader** passes (full catalogue, web included). Their findings go to one **Editor** job per finding (`verifyFinding`), and the Story's dial covers `major`, so an approved `critical`/`major` one becomes a `fix` task (D-34, D-35). **Lock Summary** is disabled while that task is open, and the open task arms round 2 — three new samples compiled from the selected draft plus the fix task (§16.7). When round 2's selection consumes the task, the fresh deep review finds nothing blocking, and the phase Goal (C-14, C-15) passes, the Story stops at `pending`/`phase_signoff`. The lock sets `summary_locked` = true, `phase` → `plotlines`, a Writer's next `editSummary` is refused naming P5, **and the tray is emptied in the lock commit** (D-36). The tray gate (L-7a) itself is unchanged, but phase ①'s shipped crew files no Ideas: the screening pass has no tools, and the deep-review pass is told to report defects rather than proposals.
2. **Phase ②.** Sweep spawns a **Writer** → reads the locked Summary and the Goal, creates exactly 8 plotlines via `createPlotline`. C-0 passes at 8 and fails at 7 or 9. A **Consistency checker** files findings (it cannot edit); the next sweep hands each finding to a fresh Writer, which edits. Goal reads `4/6 · failing: C-4, C-5`, then `6/6`.
   2a. Readers propose plotline changes; the Author selects; the loop stops at `phase_signoff`; the Author **locks the plotlines** → `phase` → `chapters`.
3. **Phase ③.** All 8 chapters enter `in_pass` together (L-8). A **Writer** on chapter 1 → `createChapter` + `editChapter`; a **Review crew** (fresh) → verdict against C-7…C-9 + `raiseFinding`; a **Reader** reads the chapter as the Author would and files "this spends the mentor's mystery two chapters early" — which lands `unconfirmed` (D-33). The next sweep gives that finding to an **Editor**, who `verifyFinding`s it true, which spawns the task; a fresh **Writer** fixes it. Chapter `done` on Author accept — or with no Author action at all (L-35).
   3a. **The false positive.** A second Reader claims chapter 3 contradicts chapter 7. The Editor checks and **disputes** it: no Writer pass is spent, and the disagreement is visible to the Author as a disputed finding rather than as work.
   3b. **The cross-chapter fix.** A confirmed finding on chapter 12 says the locket was already sold in chapter 1. Both chapters go `needs_recheck` (L-8a, L-11a); the loop fixes chapter 1, because the plotline says the sale happens there. No Author action.
   3c. **The gate.** The `major` finding auto-approves (D-35) and the Writer is on it within a sweep. A `minor` one the same Reader files sits `confirmed` in the queue, **spawning no work at all**, until the Author approves it or discards it (D-34). Flip the switch to include `minor` and the next one goes through by itself — that single toggle is the difference between steering phase ③ and watching it.
   3d. **The schema.** A Reader files a finding with no `cost`; the tool refuses naming the field, and the run re-sends it triaged (T-11). It calls `describeTool` on `raiseFinding` first and gets the enum values back — the correction is a lookup, not a guess (T-6c).
   3e. **The gap.** Give a Reader a brief that asks for something no tool reaches ("judge whether this chapter's length matches the genre norm" — there is no word-count surface). It files its finding anyway and calls `reportGap` naming what it needed and what it did instead. The gap appears on the Author's queue as a tool request, and the Story's `trend` is untouched (T-12).
4. Author hits **Stop** mid-chapter-2. Dispatch halts, the run dies, state is intact, restart resumes at chapter 2.
   4b. **The Author writes.** Mid-phase-③, the Author decides chapter 6's opening is wrong and hits **Pause & edit**: dispatch halts, in-flight runs die, and the prose pane becomes editable (D-37). Attempting the same edit without pausing is refused naming D-37. They rewrite two paragraphs, hit **Resume**, and the pane tells them what that costs: the Digest re-derived from the edit (D-12a), the chapters that depend on those paragraphs marked `needs_recheck` (D-37a, L-11a). The span is now Author-written, so the next Writer may only `proposeChange` against it (P9, D-20) — and the crew does not try to undo what the Author typed (D-37b).
   4a. **Backtrack.** On chapter 5 the Author decides act two is wrong and **backtracks to phase ②**. Chapters 1–5 and their revisions are gone; the plotlines are unlocked and editable; the verdicts and Ideas of the scrapped pass are still readable as history. Nothing in the store still references a chapter that no longer exists.

Then prove the two things that make this the product rather than a pipeline:

8. **Divergence.** Set `attempts: 3` on chapter 2 (after the backtrack, chapters are being written again). Three fresh Writers produce three drafts, invisible to each other. Checkers verdict each. An **Editor** applies the Author's `selection_rules`, picks draft 2, writes a `selection_note` naming why — and drafts 1 and 3 are **gone in the same commit**. A Writer dispatched to chapter 3 cannot find them.
   8a. **A crew definition, not an agent.** Set that Editor role to `agents: 3`. Three editor agents (executions of the one Editor employee kind) read the same three drafts inside one run and the role returns **one** selection, one verdict set, one `selection_note` — the loop sees a single report, and the crew panel shows the three nested under the run (D-38, S-7). When two of them prefer draft 1, the note says so ("2 of 3 preferred draft 1; selected 2 on the `must_happen` rule") rather than hiding the split. No Work Item is created for any of them, and no vote appears anywhere in the engine.
9. **Going in circles.** Make C-4 deliberately unfixable: two plotlines that contradict, and the crews keep working around the contradiction instead of through it. After `flat_rounds`, the Story moves to **`pending` with `pending_kind: stalled`** — a reason naming chapters 6–9 and the finding that will not close. The sweep stops touching it. The Author unlocks the plotlines, settles the contradiction, re-locks, and the Story returns to `ready` by itself.
10. **The kinds of waiting.** Put three Stories in the same list: one awaiting a lock, one **drained**, one stalled. They must be distinguishable without reading text (U-1b, U-1c, L-34) — this is the screen the Author lives in, and the first two are both "the loop is finished with something" while the third is "the loop is stuck".
11. **Drained comes first.** With the dial on `critical` + `major`, keep running phase ③. Eventually a full pass finds no task, no `unconfirmed` finding, no `competing` revision, and a fresh round of Readers produces nothing at or above `major` — while 12 `minor` and 4 `taste` findings sit open in the queue. The Story goes **`pending` with `pending_kind: drained`** and dispatches nothing (L-37). The row says what stopped ("no critical or major left · 16 findings awaiting you") and offers exactly two moves (U-1c). Approving one `minor` finding returns it to `ready` and the crew moves within a sweep; leaving it alone keeps it drained.
    11a. **Drained is not stuck.** Set `flat_rounds` low enough that this Story's flat signature would have tripped loop detection, and confirm it reads `drained` and not `stalled` (L-37a). Then raise the dial to `critical` only and watch the same Story drain sooner — the threshold that decides what counts as work is the same switch that decides when the crew stops (D-35b).
    11b. **Completion is a decision, not an empty list.** Every hard criterion passes, the Story is drained, and 16 findings plus 9 Ideas are still open. The loop proposes `completed`, and the prompt states what accepting will discard (L-25, L-25b, U-14). The Author accepts; the book is done, the leftovers close with a count against them, and the sweep stops touching it. **If the loop refuses to propose completion because the list is not empty, the build is wrong** — "nothing left to criticise" is a bar no book clears. **If it proposes completion before the Story is drained, the build is wrong in the other direction**: the crew stopped while it still had authorised work.

If step 8 feels like the Author's taste being extended rather than replaced, and step 9 feels like a helpful pause rather than a failure, the topology is right and the rest is more stages. If the Editor's pick is wrong more than half the time, fix `selection_rules` before building anything else — **that text is the product**.

## 16. Phase ① research pipeline (2026-11-23 — the LEGACY `'sampling'` mode since 2026-11-24; a new Story runs §17)

This section is normative and replaces the classic phase-① loop wherever the two disagree (§16.8 names the passages). It describes the pipeline the package ships: no flag selects it, and no classic path remains — a phase-`summary` Story always runs this order.

**D-40** **Phase ① is one pipeline: structure → research → sample → screen → select → deep review → refine.** The Seed is Author truth and is never edited (P5); the Editor writes a DERIVED `SeedBrief`; the Researcher gathers sourced `Knowledge`; N tool-less one-turn Writer samples expand the brief; M tool-less Readers screen each sample; the **engine** selects the least-issue sample and truncates the rest; the selected revision gets the tool-using deep review, the Editor's verification and the Story's auto-approve dial; the approved fix tasks arm the next sampling round, whose prompt is compiled from the selected draft plus those tasks. The pipeline's ladder is fixed: **no sample is taken before an `accepted` Seed Brief for the current Seed**, and **no sample is taken before that brief's research pass has closed — unless the Editor waived the pass** by accepting the brief with an empty `research_questions` list (D-51: the waiver is a stated editorial decision on the brief, never a heuristic about the title or the genre, and never an omitted field).

### 16.1 Order, job kinds and claim keys

| 2 | `research`       | `wi_research_<brief.rev>`              | Researcher | 0        | `researcher-story`           | full | an accepted brief for the current Seed exists, its `research_questions` list is NOT empty (an empty list WAIVES the pass — D-51, §16.9), and no CLOSED run names the key |

- **Selection is not a Work Item.** There is no `choose_sample` job and no `select` job in phase ①: the engine's `settleJob` and every sweep call the deterministic selector directly (§16.6). `seed_review` is not in `WORK_ITEM_KINDS` either — it was replaced by `structure_seed`.

- **`confirm_finding` is dormant.** The kind stays in `WORK_ITEM_KINDS` and `confirmFinding` stays in the catalogue and in §8, but no job generates either now, and the old phase-① `reader_confirmed` gates are gone: the Editor's verification of the deep review's findings is the acceptance that arms the next round.
- **The Goal is unchanged.** Phase ①'s level is still C-14 and C-15 and no new criterion was added; the Seed Brief is an input, never the contract C-14 measures against (the raw Seed is).

### 16.3 The Researcher — the fifth employee

**D-42** `researcher` is an **employee kind**, Rank A like the other four (`ROLE_KINDS` / `STORY_AGENT_RANKS`). Its template is `researcher-story` (`serves_phase: ALL_PHASES`, `web: true`, `attempts: 1`, `max_concurrent: 1`): it researches the period, place, technology and facts the Seed Brief depends on and writes one `writeKnowledge` per claim — or, when the questions the job named need no fact from outside the book, closes the pass with `reportNoResearch` (D-51). It never writes a story artifact, and a write that reaches for one is refused naming **P10**.

Its §7 row (T-1c): read the Seed and every story artifact; **write `knowledge`**; write `tasks` (it may file the fix it cannot do, D-11); READ `proposals`, `verdicts`, `findings` and `ideas` (a proposal or a judgement is an opinion about a story artifact, which the Researcher does not make; T12/F2); read `seed_briefs` and `sample_reviews`; `recommend` on `sub_agent_runs`. `GET /agents` projects a fifth row, and the roster is answered ALPHABETICALLY (Checker · Editor · Reader · Researcher · Writer) — the route sorts the harness projection, whose catalogue order appends the Researcher last (T12/F1).

**D-43** The knowledge surface is its own group, registered on **every** session like the rest of the catalogue (T-6a): `structureSeed` (editor, writes `seed_briefs`; requires `research_questions` — D-51), `writeKnowledge` (researcher, writes `knowledge`), `readKnowledge` (every kind, bounded to 40 rows per T-7), `retireKnowledge` (researcher, marks an entry `low` and appends the `(retired)` marker rather than deleting it — P11) and `reportNoResearch` (researcher only, D-51: closes a dispatched pass that found nothing to record — see §16.9). §8.7 has the argument table; T-1c and §7's rows decide who may call what.

### 16.4 The tool-less execution mode — a named exception to T-6a

**D-44** **The catalogue stays uniform; the run's offered list does not.** T-6a's principle is unchanged — every session is built with §8.1–§8.5 and §8.7, and `kind` selects the brief and the permission row — but a run may be dispatched with `toolMode: 'none'`, which is an **execution mode**: the run is offered an EMPTY tool list (the transport sends no `tools` at all), receives no tool context, and answers one assistant turn under a `response_format` contract. No permission is widened or narrowed by the mode; it is a subtraction at dispatch, and only phase ①'s `sample_unit` and `screen_samples` use it.

The transport, precisely:

- The schemas are **plain JSON Schema object literals** in `src/shared/schemas/` (`SummarySample`: premise, promise, theme, tone, ending, world_rules, knowledge_used; `IssueList`: `issues[]` whose `severity` is the enum `critical`/`high`). There is no TypeBox dependency and no schema builder.
- The request asks for `response_format: {type: 'json_schema', json_schema: {name, strict, schema}}`. If the provider rejects the field with a 4xx naming `response_format`/`json_schema`, the client retries ONCE with `{type: 'json_object'}`; the prompt still states the contract's shape (the Writer's role prompt names every field), so the downgrade is not blind. A second failure fails the run with its `end_reason`.
- The answer is parsed and validated **in process** against the same literal. There is no streaming repair (the client is non-streaming): a bad answer costs exactly ONE repair retry — the bad text is echoed back with the validator's message and path, and the model re-emits the complete object. Two provider calls are the whole budget; a second failure fails the run and persists nothing.
- The run row is created and closed exactly like any other (`spawning` → `working` → `torn_down`/`failed`), and the request/answer pair is retained in the thread log. It opens NO live engine thread, because none exists; the employee tile still counts it as one working thread, because `GET /agents` merges the harness's live engine threads with the live run rows (`src/server/run/story-engine.ts:StoryHarnessManager.mergeJournaledRuns`, keyed `run_row:<runId>`), and it also appears in the run rows and in the retained thread history.

### 16.5 Sampling and screening mechanics

**D-45** **The prompt is compiled, deterministic and byte-stable.** `src/server/run/phase1-prompt.ts` is the one compiler. Its system turn is the template role prompt plus the output contract (the schema name), identical for every sample of every round; its user turn is, in order, the **Seed Brief**, the **`critical`/`high` Knowledge** (sorted by importance rank then `fingerprint`; `normal`/`low` never enter), and — from round 2 — the **BASE REVISION** (the previous round's selected draft) and the **FIX TASKS** (open `fix` rows, sorted priority descending then id). Nothing in the text reads a clock, a run id or a revision id; the round number lives in headings only. The hash is `sha256(system + "\n\n--phase1--\n\n" + user)`, first 16 hex characters, and lands on the run row's `brief_hash` and the revision's `prompt_hash`, which is how the Author can prove two samples shared a prefix (P11). Diversity is the provider `seed`, taken from the sample's 1-based `sampleIndex`; no `temperature` is sent. The screening prompt shares the same Seed Brief and Knowledge sections and names ONE draft's fields (never its revision id); each sample's screening batch shares one system turn.

- **One structured turn per run.** `summary_sampling` (standard 3) is the number of samples a round owes; each is its own one-turn run, and each answer becomes a `drafted` revision in the commit that closes it (`origin: sampled` in round 1, `refined` later; `round` set). Each sample's `summary_readers` (standard 5) screening runs each become one `sample_reviews` row in the commit that closes them.
- **They run one at a time per family.** The sweep offers ONE sample of the in-flight round and refuses a second while a live sample run of the CURRENT WAVE exists (`wi_sample_<summary>_<round>_…`, or `…_w<wave>_…` after a re-arm), so a round's N samples are N sequential one-turn runs; screening offers one owed pass per drafted sample and holds only that sample's family. `max_concurrent` on the two templates is a ceiling, never the count (D-38a).
- **The attempt caps are implicit and load-bearing.** A round may dispatch at most `summary_sampling + 3` sampling runs PER WAVE and each sample at most `summary_readers + 3` screening runs (`records.ts:samplingCapOf`/`screeningCapOf`). A failed answer persists nothing, so the counter — not the row count — is what allows a retry; the cap is what stops a broken provider buying a run per sweep forever.
- **Total sampling failure escalates (P7), and the escalation has a real recovery (D-50).** When the in-flight round still has NO revision after its cap, the sweep opens one `escalation` task owned by the Author (title prefix "Phase ① sampling failed after", reused rather than duplicated across sweeps) saying the provider is not returning valid `SummarySample` JSON; its `detail` records the wave it was opened at (`wave: <n>`). The spent attempts stay in the run rows, so "clear this row and retry" cannot work by itself — the recovery is a WAVE: once the row is no longer `open` (the Author's clear-pending makes it `wontfix`, an answered directive makes it `done`) and the `wave: <n>` in its detail still equals `stories.sampling_wave`, the sweep bumps `sampling_wave` by one in a commit. The sample claim prefix then becomes `wi_sample_<summary>_<round>_w<wave>_`, the attempt counter starts at 1 again, and the round samples afresh; the recorded-wave guard makes the bump happen once per cleared escalation. Changing `summary_sampling` upward also re-arms without a wave (the cap moved), which is the second half of the row's own advice.
- **Screening exhaustion degrades to a partial-review selection and escalates once (D-50).** A sample that cannot collect its `summary_readers` valid reviews inside the cap counts as SCREENED (`records.ts:isFullyScreened`: full set, or attempts spent). The round therefore selects on the reviews it has — possibly none — instead of stalling forever, and the selection commit opens ONE escalation titled `Phase ① screening incomplete in round <n> — selection proceeded with partial screening` (`owner_role: 'author'`), whose detail names the sample(s), the cap, and states that selection proceeded. Screens have no wave: the degrade is their recovery.

**D-46** **The engine is what writes the sample.** No tool call persists a sample or a screening answer: the engine reads the run's validated structured answer and writes the `revisions` / `sample_reviews` row itself, on the same named-exception principle as D-12a's Digest (P10's second exception). The run row, the template and the assignment are what attribute the prose to the Writer.

### 16.6 Selection and truncation

**D-47** **Selection is a deterministic engine settlement, not a model call and not a Work Item.** It runs after every `sample_unit`/`screen_samples` settlement and once per sweep (so a restart that died between the last screen and the selection still finishes the round). It fires only when: the in-flight round `R = (stories.sampling_round ?? 0) + 1` has at least one revision; no revision of `R` is already `selected`; at least one revision is `drafted`; the round OWES NO MORE SAMPLES — `roundRevisions(R).length >= summary_sampling`, OR the CURRENT WAVE's sample attempts reached `summary_sampling + 3` (degrade to the survivors only once sampling is genuinely exhausted, never while more samples are owed), OR the round's sampling LEG has closed (D-52: the cap is spent, or the round STARTED and the arming rule turned it off mid-flight — `loop/records.ts:samplingLegClosed`); every `drafted` revision is SCREENED — its full `summary_readers` set, OR its screening attempts reached `summary_readers + 3` (the partial-selection degrade of §16.5); and no live sample/screen run remains.

The ordering is fixed and there is no score (P6): **fewest `critical` → fewest `high` → fewest total → the earliest sample** (creation order; ties in that order fall back to id).

In ONE commit it then (opening the Author's `Phase ① screening incomplete in round R …` escalation first when any drafted revision was screened only partially, per §16.5):

1. installs the winner's **six prose fields** — `premise`, `promise`, `theme`, `tone`, `ending`, `world_rules` — on the Summary, and **never `chapter_count`** (Author truth, D-3b);
2. marks the winner `selected` and records its `issue_counts`;
3. appends the round's line to `stories.sampling_history` BEFORE any delete, so the audit can name every competitor;
4. **hard-deletes** every other revision of the round and every `sample_reviews` row pointing at one (P13/D-22's truncation, as the request asked) — and, since D-52, every `drafted`/`competing` Summary draft of an EARLIER round too: a draft that was still being screened when an earlier selection ran escaped that commit's truncation, and nothing else ever removes it. The retirement takes the finding that named ONLY a retired draft with it (`discarded`) and the open task that finding queued (`wontfix`), so a defect about a text the Summary no longer holds cannot hold the sign-off; a finding naming no draft, or a draft that survives, is left alone (D-53);
5. marks every open `fix` task `done` — the round's prompt carried it, so the resample IS its answer — and closes each finding it named, still `confirmed`/`approved`, as `fixed` (the rule a classic fix pass applies). A resample that did not fix the defect re-files the same fingerprint on the new draft, which L-16a reads as a regression rather than trusting this write;
6. advances `stories.sampling_round` to `R`.

The losing rows are gone from the store. `sampling_history` is the stored audit line; the Story view can still RENDER a deleted loser because the journal kept the delete payload and the projection marks it `discarded` — a reading surface, not a surviving row (P11).

**D-48** **The round counter and its resets.** `stories.sampling_round` means "rounds completed"; the in-flight round is that number plus one. A backtrack to `summary` and an Author `editSeed` both scrap every revision and reset `sampling_round` to 0, `sampling_history` to empty and `sampling_wave` to 0, because all three describe samples the same commit deleted. Without the reset the reopened phase would sample "round N+1" against a base revision that no longer exists.

### 16.7 The refinement gate, the deep review, and the stops

**D-49** **Round 2+ is armed by approved work, not by a calendar.** Round 1 samples as soon as gates 1-2 are closed. A refinement round is offered only when BOTH hold: at least one `fix` task is `open`, and no `critical`/`major` finding is still `unconfirmed` (an unverified complaint is not an instruction — D-33). Its prompt carries the previous round's selected draft and those tasks (§16.5). If the Story's dial does not cover the confirmed costs, no task is created; the Story waits at `pending`/`finding_approval` until the Author approves one (D-34).

**D-52** **The arming rule gates NEW samples; it never strands a round that already sampled.** A refinement round's leg can close under itself — a review files an unconfirmed `critical`/`major` while the round's first draft is still being screened, and D-49's arming rule then refuses the next sample. Two facts are read separately, from the ledger, and deliberately at DIFFERENT scopes: the cap arm counts the CURRENT WAVE's sample attempts (`records.ts:sampleAttempts` at `stories.sampling_wave`, the re-arm contract of T12/E), while the started arm asks the ROUND question wave-agnostically (`records.ts:samplingRoundStarted` counts any `wi_sample_<summary>_<round>_*` key via `sampleRoundPrefixOf`, current wave or not — a wave bump is a retry of the same round, so the drafts made before it are still this round's and a started round that cannot close would strand them). the round had NOT started (D-49's release stands and the round is not selected), and the round HAD started (its sampling leg is closed, its drafts are screened and reviewed, and the selection proceeds on the drafts that exist). Refusing the second case is what stranded a round: §16.6's sample-set arm was never satisfied, the sign-off was gated on a selection that could not happen, and the loop waited on work it had itself stopped buying. Screening keeps running for the active round's drafts regardless of arming — what the leg closing cancels is a NEW sample, not the passes an existing draft owes — and the sign-off is still gated on D-33 (the unconfirmed finding holds the lock) and the next round is still gated on D-49, so this is a shorter round, not a weaker gate.

**D-53** **A finding belongs to ONE draft, and the loop may be sent at it only so many times.** Phase ① reads a finding's owner from `related_refs` (`loop/records.ts:findingRevisionScope`), and the answer is computed once for the sign-off's blocking set, the verification queue, the drain probe and the selection's retirement (D-52 step 4), so no two gates can disagree about which draft a complaint describes:

- **`selected`** — it names the draft the Summary row carries. Normal work: it blocks, and it is verified.
- **`superseded`** — every ref names a draft this Story has already had (the revision row, or the round's `sampling_history` line, still names it) and none of them is the carried draft. The defect was written about a text the Summary no longer holds: it stays `unconfirmed` on the record for the Author, and it neither blocks a gate nor buys another verification pass, because the pass re-reads the KEPT draft and can never confirm or dispute it.
- **`artifact`** — it names no draft, or names something that is not a Summary draft (the Summary row itself, a criterion, a chapter). L-11a's whole-Story shape keeps the blocking weight it always had; it is never silently dropped, and an unknown id counts as `artifact`, never as `superseded`.

Because that reading is the only attribution a finding carries, phase ① ASKS FOR IT AT FILING: `raiseFinding` on a Summary target, in phase `summary`, once a selection has installed a draft, is refused naming **D-33** (`field: 'related_refs'`) unless `related_refs` names the carried revision — with the id it wants in `expected`, and a distinct sentence when the refs name only set-aside drafts. The three shapes the rule is NOT about stay free: a `stories`-targeted whole-Story finding, a chapter/plotline finding, and a filing before the round's first selection.

And the queue is BOUNDED. Phase ① mints `wi_verify_finding_<id>_<n>` — one anchored family per finding, the trailing `_` so `finding_1`'s budget is never spent by `finding_10` — and stops offering passes once the family reached **`FINDING_VERIFY_CAP`** (one pass plus `SAMPLING_RETRY_ALLOWANCE` retries, 4 today). A verification run that closes without deciding anything leaves the finding `unconfirmed`, and an unconfirmed finding is a D-33 hold, so an uncapped queue bought the same question on every sweep forever: never drained, never stalled, never explained. On the sweep after the budget is spent the loop opens ONE Author escalation — `Phase ① verification undecided for finding <id> …`, `owner_role: 'author'`, `finding_ref` set, and state **`blocked`** rather than `open`, because an `open` row counts as dispatchable work and would leave the Story `ready` with nothing to run, while a `blocked` row is the shape the decision tree renders as "the crew asked you" (P7, L-36). The sweep closes the row (`done`) as soon as the finding is not stalled any more — the Editor answered it, the Author decided it, or the round retired the draft that carried it. The closing is NOT phase-scoped even though the opening is: `findingsVerifyStalled` returns nothing outside phase ①, so a row whose finding gets decided after the Story moves on can only be retired by a close pass that still runs. And the stalled list excludes two findings beyond the superseded-draft case: one whose last bought verification pass is still live (`records.ts:verifyFindingLive` — the budget counts dispatched runs, so opening the Author's question over the head of an Editor run that has not answered would buy a decision one commit late) and one scoped to a set-aside draft. `records.ts:findingsVerifyStalled` is the one list both halves read, so generation and escalation cannot disagree about which findings stalled. The reuse lookup that keeps the row at ONE per finding matches its `finding_ref`, and the title prefix it matches on is anchored with the ` —` separator: `finding_1` is a text prefix of `finding_10`, and a bare id prefix would hand one finding's question to another finding's sweep.

**Deep review.** The selected revision of the round that just completed gets `summary_readers` tool-using Reader passes, one per `wi_review_<selected>_a<pass>` key, rotating over the four built-in angles (`reader-genre`, `reader-plausibility`, `reader-audience`, `reader-mystery`). These runs hold the whole catalogue and may use the web; their findings — at any cost — go to one `verify_findings` Editor job PER finding (`wi_verify_finding_<id>_<n>`), which binds `editor-general` because it is the FIRST Editor template in `DEFAULT_ASSIGNMENT` — a job that names no template takes the first assigned template of its role kind. The dial then auto-approves what it covers, and the approved finding's `fix` task is what arms the next round.

**Sign-off.** Phase ①'s `phase_signoff` is offered only when the Goal passes AND all of: no competing revision (D-52: a Summary draft of a round the loop has already chosen past is not competition — it is a leftover, and it must not hold the lock forever), no `pending` draft, no sourced Idea awaiting verification, no unconfirmed `critical`/`major` finding (D-53: a finding about a set-aside draft is not this round's work), no phase-① `fix`/`rewrite`/`merge` task queued, no `critical`/`major` finding open on the selected revision, every deep-review pass the selected revision OWES closed (the debt exists only for the revision that IS the current selection — `records.ts:isDeepReviewSelection` reads the same condition the `wi_review_*` queue does, so an older-round draft picked from the Summary pane cannot owe passes the loop will never dispatch and hold the lock on a debt nobody can pay), the phase not yet locked, and the Ideas tray empty (L-7a). The lock itself is unchanged: signing sets `summary_locked`, moves the phase, mirrors `summaries.locked`, and empties the tray (D-36).

**Drain.** The phase-① drain preconditions add the pipeline's own stages to L-37's four: an accepted Seed Brief for the CURRENT Seed; a closed research pass for that brief; no live sample/screen; the in-flight round either complete (selected, so no `drafted` sample is left) or deliberately not owed (the round is only "owed" while it is armed, and per D-52 a round whose leg has CLOSED is owed its selection, not further samples); and every deep-review pass the selected revision owes closed (`records.ts:isDeepReviewSelection` — a selection from an earlier round owes none, and the drain must not name work the queue cannot offer). A round that degraded (spent sampling cap, spent screening cap) counts as complete once its selection committed. Drain is still tested before stalled and wins (L-37a).

**Removed from the loop, kept in the vocabulary.** `confirm_finding` (kind) and `confirmFinding` (tool) are no longer dispatched at all — phase ① or otherwise — and `reader_confirmed` no longer gates a fix, a selection, a sign-off or the drain. The tool still exists and still behaves as §8 documents for any template that reaches for it.

## 17. The Summary Phrase — grouped authoring (2026-11-24, the model every new Story runs)

**Status: normative, and it supersedes §16 for a Story whose `stories.summary_mode` is `'groups'` — which is every Story created since this date (`loop/engine.ts:createStory`, §4.1).** §16 is not deleted and is not decorative: it stays the contract for a legacy `'sampling'` book (mode ABSENT means `'sampling'`, R6) and for the later-phase backend this redesign does not touch. Where the two sections disagree about phase ①, the Story's mode decides which one runs, and the ONE predicate that answers that question is `phrase/catalog.ts:isGroupManagedStory` — every guard in the loop, the tool layer and the routes reads it from there, and nothing may re-derive the answer from the presence of rows.

**Why the redesign happened, in one paragraph.** §16's whole-Summary round asks N Writers to rewrite all six prose fields end to end, then deletes N−1 of them (D-22). One sentence of criticism therefore costs a rewrite of everything; the deleted drafts survive only as a line in `sampling_history`; a "Revision 1 … N" rail cannot say *Tone is fixed and Premise still isn't*; and a Writer budget spent re-drafting a whole book cannot be spent on the part that is actually wrong. The grouped model keeps §16's gates, its evidence rules and its reading economy, and changes the **unit of the work**: the Summary is five named parts, each with its own revision line, installed on acceptance, reviewed as one vector, and answered by findings that name the parts they are about.

### 17.1 The requirement ids (R1–R7)

### 17.2 Order, job kinds and claim keys

```
the Editor structures the Seed    (structure_seed)  → seed_briefs: one accepted Seed Brief     [§16, unchanged]
the Researcher gathers Knowledge  (research)        → knowledge: sourced, importance-triaged   [§16, unchanged]
one tool-less Writer pass         (draft_groups)    → group_revisions: a `latest` + an `archived` row PER GROUP it wrote
                                                      + phrases.content (the Overview) + §4.2's mirror  (R2/R5/R6)
N tool-less reading seats         (review_groups)   → sample_reviews rows carrying a GroupReview, every issue with group_refs
the deep review with tools        (check_unit)      → findings that MUST name their groups (R3)
the Editor verifies               (verify_findings) → confirm / dispute                        [§16, unchanged]
the dial auto-approves            (D-35)            → a `fix` task, and the task's groups are what the NEXT batch is built from
```

| job | claim key | how many, and when |
| -- | -- | -- |
| `draft_groups` (Writer, `toolMode: 'none'`) | `wi_draft_groups_<summaryRef>_<group-set hash>_<attempt>` — the FAMILY is the group set, so a failed pass retries under the next attempt number and a restart reproduces the family exactly (L-3) | one per sweep while at least one group is owed, unheld, and inside its allowance; the batch is `max(1, stories.summary_sampling)` groups, cut by R4's exclusion |
| `review_groups` (Reader, `toolMode: 'none'`) | `wi_review_groups_<vectorHash>_<pass>` | `stories.summary_readers` seats per **group vector**; one run per seat per vector, and a seat that already reported on this state is not bought again |
| `check_unit` (Reader, tools ON) | `wi_group_check_<vectorHash>_<pass>` | the same count, rotating the four deep-review Reader angles (`reader-genre`, `reader-plausibility`, `reader-audience`, `reader-mystery`); opens only once every structured seat for that vector has reported |

The `draft_groups` job answers `GroupDraft` = `{groups: [{group_ref, content}], knowledge_used: []}`; a reading seat answers `GroupReview` = `{issues: [{severity: 'critical' | 'high', criterion, location, quote, detail, fix_hint?, group_refs: […]}], fix_verifications?: […]}`. `severity` keeps §16's two legacy words because every "blocking" predicate in the loop reads exactly that pair; `origin` is GONE from the review contract, because introduced-vs-inherited graded a CANDIDATE against a base, and a grouped answer is installed rather than compared. `group_refs` is required and non-empty **in process** as well as in schema: `parseGroupReviewAnswer` refuses an empty list and refuses a ref that is not one of the Story's groups, so an invented id is a refusal, not a dangling attribution.

**Which groups owe work** (`loop/work.ts:groupWorkTargets`, read from records so a restart reproduces it — L-3): a group with `revision: 0` (never written), or a group an open finding targets that no revision of THAT group resolves (`findings.ts:findingGroupsStillOwed`). A finding with no `group_refs` — possible only on a legacy row, since R3 refuses a new one — targets all five. That is the whole routing rule; §17.6 is what trims it.

### 17.3 What a grouped Story writes, and what it never writes

| written by the group path | never written by the group path |
| -- | -- |

### 17.4 The install: one commit, and the six things it does

1. one new `group_revisions` row **per group the batch actually wrote**, `number` = that group's own next number (R2);
2. the row each of those replaced flipped to `archived` — under a CAS on the group's `latest`, so exactly one current text per group survives an attempted concurrent install;
3. `phrase_groups.content` / `.revision` / `.updated_by` rewritten from the same text, in the same commit, so "what is the current premise" is one row read and cannot drift;
4. `phrases.content` recomposed from the group rows **as they now are** (`composePhraseContent`, §4.19) and `phrases.revision` bumped once;
5. §4.2's five prose fields written from the same texts through the identity mirror (R6) — a reader can never see a new group beside an old mirror;
6. **never** `chapter_count`, **never** `world_rules`; and if the Summary row is `locked`, step 5 is SKIPPED rather than failed — the install stands, the mirror waits for the Author to lift the lock, and `review.mirror: {summary_id, in_sync, locked, fields}` reports the difference (L-7a/P5).

**The independence example, normative (R2).** A Reader files a finding about the Promise's stakes. The batch rewrites Promise alone. Promise goes **revision 1 → 2**, the Overview recomposes, the mirror's `promise` moves — and **Premise, Theme, Tone and Ending are still at revision 1**, with their text, their number and their `updated_by` untouched, no new rows, no allowance spent, and their `writer_busy` flags never flipped. "One group's work never revises another's" is a store-level fact, not an instruction to a model.

### 17.5 The group vector, and why review re-arms by itself

The accepted state of a grouped Summary is `groupVectorHash`: sha256-16 over the sorted `<group id>:<number>` pairs, so the same set of texts in any order hashes the same, and **any install moves the hash**. Consequences that are normative:

- the reading seats and the deep review are keyed by the vector, not by a round number, so a new text re-arms exactly the review of THAT text and a restart reproduces the keys from the rows (L-3);
- a seat's job is the Overview as installed — its `group_refs` carry **all five** groups, because a defect that crosses groups must be able to name every group it crosses;
- the rail reports `review.vector_hash`, `reviews_on_current_vector` and `deep_reviews_on_current_vector` so "has anyone read what is on the record?" is a fact on the wire, not a guess.

### 17.6 Dispatch: exclusion, parallelism, and the two attempts (R4)

- **The hold is a predicate over rows, not a semaphore and not a lease.** `phrase/exclusion.ts:liveGroupSets` reads the live `runs` rows of the holding family (`GROUP_HOLDING_KINDS = ['draft_groups']`); the S-5 ceiling, the lease and L-2's expiry are untouched. Being clock-free, two engines asking in the same instant agree (L-5).
- **Fail closed.** A live holding run with no `group_refs` holds EVERY group: one deferred sweep costs less than two writers installing over each other and an archived revision nobody read.
- **Shrink, never cancel.** `partitionGroupRefs` splits a candidate batch into `{free, deferred}` (the rows form is `partitionGroups`), so `{premise, tone}` with premise already live still offers `{tone}` this sweep. Cancelling the batch would make an exclusion into a serialiser and idle the queue while one group's writer works. A wholly held batch offers nothing — and no claim key is spent on an empty answer. **Disjoint batches run concurrently.**
- **Two attempts per TEXT — not a lifetime ceiling.** `GROUP_WRITE_ATTEMPT_CAP = 2`, counted over CLOSED holding runs whose JOURNAL POSITION is after the write that produced the group's current text: `writeBaselineOf` takes that text's `latest` revision row when one exists, and otherwise the LAST create-or-update event on the `phrase_groups` row. So a successful install MOVES the baseline and RESETS that group's count (and only that group — R2), which means a group that was rewritten cleanly and is criticised again has a fresh pair of attempts against the NEW text; a live run is not counted; a `review_groups` or deep-review run is never counted, because **reading the Overview does not spend the budget for rewriting it**; the pass that installed the current text predates its own revision row and so is not an attempt AGAINST it, which is why a freshly written group starts with its full pair in front of it; and a Seed reset (`engine.ts:editSeed`) re-baselines through the reset's own update event, so the scrapped Seed's passes are never charged to the new one. The baseline being a JOURNAL POSITION rather than a row id is load-bearing: `createdAfter` resolves ids through a row's CREATE event, which for a group row predates every write the allowance counts, and counting from that create after a reset would park every group and let an EMPTY Overview read as drained.
- **The escalation is the finding, not a new question — and a parked group is not owed.** When a group's allowance is spent the loop stops paying for that group and the finding stays open in the review rail with its attempts visible; the Author's moves are to decide the finding or rewrite the group by hand. The loop parks the group rather than asking the Author a second question about the same defect, and it never installs a least-bad text. `groupPipelineOwed` deliberately does NOT count a parked group: a cap that also held the drain would mean a provider that cannot answer the `GroupDraft` schema keeps the Story from ever reading `drained`, and the stall would look like a hang.

### 17.7 A finding's groups, and when one may close (R3)

- **attribution is checked against the book** before any row moves: an unknown group id is refused (never dropped), and a phase-① Summary finding with no `group_refs` on a grouped book is refused with the `key=id` list in the message — a finding that names none means "the whole Overview", and that is five groups rewritten to answer one sentence. The refusal's `fix` BRANCHES by book: a legacy whole-Summary Story is told it may omit the list (there, an empty list really does mean the whole Overview), while a grouped Story is told to name every id, because **a grouped Summary has no unscoped path to omit into** — closure is a join on the ids (`findings.ts:findingGroupScope`). The legacy D-33 rule that a phase-① Summary finding must name the draft it describes is skipped on a group book: there is no competing draft there, so the id that rule demands does not exist (R5);
- **a resolving revision is an explicit claim**: `finding_refs` on the `group_revisions` row, frozen at dispatch so a late task cannot claim an older text. Newest such row wins;
- **all-or-nothing closure**: `findingResolution` → `{targeted, resolved, missing, resolved_all}`, and `closeFindingWhenGroupsResolved` flips the finding to `fixed` only when `resolved_all` AND the row is `approved` (D-34: an unapproved row must not close over the Author's gate; auto-approve writes `approved` with `approved_by: 'auto'`, so covered costs close without a tap). It is idempotent, so a replayed install after a restart changes nothing;
- **a repeat sighting widens the scope** instead of filing a second claim (D-16's dedupe merges `group_refs` the way it merges `related_refs`);
- **the sweep reconciles what the installs owe, on group books only.** The planner cuts a batch off an OPEN finding (`groupWorkTargets` accepts D-33's `unconfirmed`/`confirmed` rows on purpose), so a pass can install the answer BEFORE any of the three approval funnels writes `approved` — the Editor's `approveFinding`, the engine's approval commit, the dial's auto-approve. The install-time close then correctly declines, and because no group still owes the finding the planner never dispatches again, so the row used to sit `approved` and unanswered forever. The sweep re-runs the same read every round (`engine.ts:560-595`, the `loop_reconcile_findings` step → `findings.ts:closeFindingAnsweredByInstalls`, which RE-READS the row rather than trusting the caller's pre-update copy) and closes the fix tasks that named the now-`fixed` finding, so a defect the book can already show as answered never holds L-37's drain hostage. The step is gated on `isGroupManagedStory` for an honest reason: with no phrase rows `findingResolution` answers `resolved_all: true` VACUOUSLY — nothing was ever in scope — and acting on that would delete a sampling round's fix work. A reconcile step rather than a write at each approval, because a crash between any funnel and the close has to self-heal anyway (L-3).

**The multi-group example, normative.** A Checker files "the Ending resolves a confrontation the Premise never set up" with `group_refs: [premise, ending]`. It buys **one** `draft_groups` pass over both groups (their still-owed sets are unioned into one batch when both are free), the install stamps `finding_refs: [that finding]` on **both** new revisions, and the finding stays open while `missing` is non-empty. Closing it when only Ending moved would re-open the identical complaint on the next sweep — which is the loop's own definition of no progress. A partial fix is a STEP, not a stall: `findingGroupsStillOwed` routes exactly the missing groups into the next batch. (`integration/summary-groups.test.ts` is that example end to end.)

**The wider example: three groups, a batch of one, and a late approval.** On a book whose `summary_sampling` is 1, a finding naming THREE groups is fixed over three sweeps, and because §4.10 has no "partly fixed" state the row stays `approved` with its task `open` until the LAST named group installs a claiming revision — closing on the first install would report a defect as answered while two of the five groups still said the thing the Author complained about. If the approval arrives AFTER the last install, the install-time close declines (the state gate is exactly the field the funnel has not written yet), the planner has nothing left to dispatch, and the sweep's reconcile step closes the finding and its tasks one round later. Pinned by `loop/group-findings-batch-cap.test.ts`, including the vacuous legacy read and the refusal to act on it.

### 17.8 Gates, sign-off, the drain, and the drain's honest limit

- **The HTTP lock gate is unchanged**: the Ideas tray first (L-7a/D-29), then the phase Goal (U-16). The redesign added no server-side blocker to that route and no new `pending_kind`.
- **The desk adds the group truth to what it SHOWS** (U-19), from `story-contract.ts:groupSignOffBlockers` in five sentences: the phrase read has not landed or failed; a group is unwritten (named by title); an open blocking-cost finding sits on a group (named by the groups it crosses); the queue holds an open task (phrased as a STORY task, because the read's task rows carry no group attribution and a chapter repair holds the Summary sign-off too — that IS the engine's queued-work rule); a live Writer is rewriting a group, so the text on screen is about to move. It may not show a blocker the lock route ignores, and it may not wave through a five-group Summary the rows say is half-written.
- **The drain reads the group pipeline, and both gates read ONE predicate.** `loop/status.ts:phase1Unmet` has two branches and never runs both: a grouped book answers from `phrase/exclusion.ts:groupPipelineOwed` — the same gates `work.ts:phase1GroupCandidates` builds candidates from — and a `'sampling'` book answers from the sampling families (§16.7). The grouped sentences, in §6.4's order, are exactly: `"…group-pipeline run(s) still in flight (R5, §6.4 steps 3-6)"` (live runs matched by `isGroupPipelineRun`), `"…group(s) still owe the Overview a draft or a fix pass (R2, §6.4 step 3)"`, `"…Reader pass(es) still owed on the installed group Overview (R3, R5, §6.4 step 4)"`, and `"…deep-review pass(es) still owed for the installed group Overview (§6.4 step 6, R5)"`. The seat keys the probe counts are the GENERATOR's keys (`exclusion.ts:groupReviewSeatKey` / `groupCheckSeatKey`), so the queue and the probe cannot spell one seat two ways. Pinned stage-by-stage by `loop/phase1-groups-drain.test.ts`.
- **Sign-off is held by the same read, and the gate got TIGHTER, never looser.** `status.ts:decideStory` withholds the `pending / phase_signoff` OFFER while the CURRENT vector's structured seats or deep passes are owed (`groupOwedForSignoff.structuredReviews + deepReviews > 0`). The legacy clause it replaces — "deep passes owed on the SELECTED Summary revision" — answers zero unconditionally on a group book, because a group book has no selected Summary revision; leaving it meant the sweep offered the lock over an Overview nobody had finished reading (L-29 signed a verdict that was not complete). Because `groupPipelineOwed` counts the seats of the CURRENT vector first and only then the deep passes, **an accepted install re-arms a whole reading cycle**: `summary_readers` `review_groups` passes, then that many `check_unit` passes, before the Story may read `drained` or be offered a sign-off. That is the honest reading of "the crew still has work": the text moved, so the reading is owed again.
- **The engine skips the sampling machinery** for a grouped book: no selection (`engine.ts:1606`), no wave re-arm (`1994`), no plateau audit (`2073`), and the planner's mode switch never reaches the sampler (`work.ts:1261-1262`). The legacy plateau STOP still gates a legacy book, minus the Author escalation and the wave bump R5 removed.
- **Deleting a Story deletes its Phrase rows** (the deviation this section recorded on 2026-11-24 is closed): `engine.ts:2673-2675` purges `phrases`, `phrase_groups` and `group_revisions` in the same `delete_story` commit as the Story row, after the live runs are killed with `'delete'` and settled, with no lock exemption (P1: the whole book goes). Phrase findings, tasks and group-review verdicts ride the pre-existing `findings`/`tasks`/`verdicts` deletes. The measurement that found it survives as provenance — `Temp/create-storyline-orchestrator/T4/phrase-delete-probe.ts`, run through `yarn vite-node`, reported `phrases=1 phrase_groups=5` before the fix. Pinned by `api/story-delete.test.ts`.
- **Scrap, the Seed edit and the backtrack are group-aware now** (D-63, §17.12):
  - `loop/invalidation.ts:scrapPhasesAfter` deletes the story's `group_revisions` rows whenever the scrapped rank is below `chapters` and the book is group-managed, and REPORTS the number as `counts.groupRevisions` beside `revisions`/`ideas`/`digests`/`verdicts`. The reasons are all three of the joins a stale `latest` row keeps: the write allowance counts "runs after THIS text" from it (`exclusion.ts:writeBaselineOf`), the review seats are keyed to the vector its number hashes into, and R3's closure joins on claims stamped onto exactly these rows.
  - `loop/engine.ts:editSeed` (3111-3135) resets the five `phrase_groups` rows IN PLACE — `content: ''`, `revision: 0`, `updated_by: null`, **same UUIDs** — and the `phrases` row to `content: ''`, `revision: 0`, with the mirror cleared beside them, so the next Writer batch is the five unwritten groups again and `ensurePhraseCatalog` re-pressed is a no-op (R1's addresses outlive the text).
  - `loop/engine.ts:backtrack('summary')` keeps L-31's convention rather than inventing a new one: the reopened phase's OWN artifact survives (group texts and their mirror stay in sync as the base for another round) while the revision HISTORY goes with the scrapped work and the sampling counters restart. "Reset the current text" is the Seed edit's call; "keep the text, drop the history" is the backtrack's — the same split the legacy prose flow already made, now applied to the group rows.
  Pinned by `loop/group-seed-reset.test.ts` (next batch = the five unwritten groups; the catalog re-press is a no-op; a sibling Story's catalog is untouched).
- **The drain's honest limit, restated.** It never advances while the group pipeline owes — live runs, owed drafts, unreported seats, unrun deep passes — and the two things it does NOT claim are the honest edges: a group whose write allowance is spent is PARKED and not owed (a provider that cannot answer the schema must not make the Story undrainable), and the unmet list names groups and seats, never a per-criterion verdict, which stays the Goal panel's job.

### 17.10 Prompts (R7, and what is already fixed)

The compiled prompts stay byte-stable per state and carry the prompt hash onto the revision row (D-44/D-45's cache rule is unchanged). A group draft prompt carries the Seed Brief (or the Seed, P5), the `critical`/`high` Knowledge triage, the group definitions quoted from the catalog, the CURRENT text of the groups in the batch (a revision is a revision OF a text — a Writer handed only tasks would rewrite a group it cannot see), and the FIX TASKS block **only when the batch carries any**: D-34's approved findings reach a batch as the tasks they became, and a first draft is handed none, because an empty heading reads as an unanswered demand. A reading seat's prompt carries the composed Overview — all five groups with their revision numbers, the combination under review, which is the key the job was claimed under — and the still-open fix tasks to grade. The binding rewrite/rate sentences live in the compiler (`GROUP_DRAFT_INSTRUCTION` / `GROUP_REVIEW_INSTRUCTION`), so the brief and the cache key come from one place; **the final role-level instruction wording is R7's later task** and is deliberately not claimed here.

Added by the same-day hardening, so the code and the spec cite the same thing:

- **D-61 — the grouped write path is closed at the TOOL layer, and every refusal carries a grouped `fix`.** `editSummary` → `R6`, Summary-unit `selectRevision` → `R5`, grouped Summary `raiseFinding` → `R3`, all keyed on `isGroupManagedStory`; the hint names the `draft_groups` job, the per-group revise route, or `raiseFinding` with the group ids, and never the retired selection (§17.9, §17.11).
- **D-62 — drain, sign-off and queue are ONE read.** `phrase/exclusion.ts:groupPipelineOwed` is the single source for live group runs, owed drafts/fix passes, the CURRENT vector's Reader seats and its deep passes; `phase1Unmet`, `decideStory` and the work generator all read it, the seat keys are the generator's keys, a parked group is not owed, and an install re-arms a full reading cycle (§17.8).
- **D-63 — the group book is purged and reset like every other artifact, and its identity survives the reset.** `deleteStory` purges the three Phrase collections in its one commit; `editSeed` resets the five rows in place with the same UUIDs (`''`/`0`/null) and clears the Overview and mirror; `scrapPhasesAfter` clears the revision HISTORY and reports `counts.groupRevisions`; a backtrack keeps the texts and drops the history, per L-31.
